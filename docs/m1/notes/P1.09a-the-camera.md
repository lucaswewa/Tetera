## Summary

Tetera has a camera interface that a driver, the virtual camera, a fake or a bridge can each implement, and a suite that proves an implementation keeps it.
- **The fake camera passes the contract suite, and a fake broken on purpose fails it,** as the phase's *Done when* asks. A fake whose `stop` didn't stop its thread failed with "a frame came after stop returned".
- **The suite has tests of its own:** a camera flawed on purpose, in one way for each check, fails that check. The break on purpose stays as a test.
- **`Camera`:**
  - capabilities, and a configuration checked against them, saying exactly what is wrong;
  - streaming into a `FrameSink` from a pool the driver owns, snaps, a software trigger;
  - events to each subscriber.

  Capabilities, configurations and events serialise, so the same trait works over a bridge (R1).
- **[ADR-0013](../../adr/0013-the-hardware-abstraction-layer.md)** records the HAL.

## What was built

- **`tetera-hal`,** a new crate in layer 2:
  - `camera`: `CameraCapabilities` (modes, binnings, ROI steps, exposure and gain spans, triggers), `default_config`, `check` and `layout`; `CameraConfig`; `CameraEvent`; `FrameSink`, for any closure that takes a frame; and the `Camera` trait;
  - `event`: `Events`, a channel for each subscriber, the gone let go;
  - `fake`: `FakeCamera`, modes of 640 × 480 (12 or 8 bits) and 160 × 120 (8 or 12 bits) of an RGGB sensor, a moving test pattern at a set rate from a pool of 4 frames, timestamped by a `Clock`; `CameraFault`: a refused configuration, dropped frames, a failed stream, a disconnection and its return;
  - `contract::camera`: seven checks every camera must pass, and `all`, which runs each on a fresh camera.
- **The `hal-fakes` example.**
- **No new dependencies.**

## Design notes and deviations from the plan

- **The camera pushes; the sink may keep.** A streaming camera hands each frame to its sink and moves on. A sink that keeps frames empties the camera's pool, and the camera then drops frames, emits `Dropped`, and says on its next frame how many were lost. A slow consumer can't stall the camera.
- **`stop` is a promise:** once it returns, no frame reaches the sink. The fake's thread checks its stop flag again just before it delivers, and `stop` waits for the thread.
- **`Busy` while streaming:** `start`, `configure` and `snap`. A snap while streaming could take the stream's next frame instead; nothing needs it yet.
- **A software trigger, but no external one:** the capabilities can declare an external trigger, but the fake offers none, and the suite tests the software trigger alone.
- **The contract suite is public code that panics,** not `#[cfg(test)]`, so the virtual camera's crate (P1.12) and the drivers (M5) can run it from their own tests.
- **The fake's pattern** is `(x + y + n) × 16` in 12 bits for frame n, so each frame differs from the last and its values can be checked by hand.
- **The fake's small mode lists 8 bits first,** so the suite's snap, in the last mode at its first bit depth, differs from the default configuration in every setting it checks.

## Tests, and the mutants

- **20 tests,** in every build:
  - **capabilities:** the default configuration, and the whole sensor's layout; an ROI and binning shaping the frames; each thing wrong with a configuration said, 15 cases; capabilities, configurations and events through JSON;
  - **events:** each subscriber getting the events after it came, the gone let go;
  - **the fake:**
    - the whole contract suite;
    - the pattern to the last pixel of the last row, the clock's timestamps and each frame's number, cut and binned;
    - each fault;
    - a sink that keeps every frame, and the drops it causes, counted on the next frame;
    - the stream's rate: the 20th frame never sooner than its time;
    - dropping the camera stops its stream;
  - **the suite's own tests:** a camera flawed on purpose fails each check. The flaws:
    - it has no modes;
    - it ignores its mode, bit depth, gain or exposure;
    - it refuses a bad configuration as a fault;
    - it hands frames on late, after `stop` returns;
    - it fails to stop when not streaming;
    - it gives two frames for each trigger.

    One more runs `all` on a camera whose last check fails, so `all` runs every check.
- **The contract suite's checks:**
  - sound capabilities, through JSON;
  - every mode at every bit depth configured and read back, each saying so;
  - bad configurations refused, changing nothing;
  - a snap of the configured layout and metadata;
  - a stream in order, refusing what it can't do meanwhile, and nothing after `stop`;
  - stopping when not streaming;
  - one frame for each software trigger.
- **Broken on purpose,** as the spec asks: **a fake camera that streams on after it is stopped.** The suite caught it: "a frame came after stop returned". It stays as a test, the flaw *hands frames on late*.
- **Run 15 times,** the tests passed each time.
- **Mutants:** 146 in the phase's diff; **118 caught, 28 unviable, none missed.**
  - **The first run missed 26:**
    - 19 in the contract suite, each a check emptied or weakened that a sound camera still passes. The flawed camera's tests were written for them, and a camera ignoring one setting at a time catches each setting dropped from a check.
    - The fake's row width: the last pixel the test read was 0, the same in a buffer never written. The test now reads a last pixel of 352.
    - The fake's rate: nothing timed the stream. The new rate test catches a period of 1 s and a schedule that never waits.
    - `Drop`: the new test drops a streaming camera.
  - **Three exclusions, each with its reason in `.cargo/mutants.toml`:**
    - the fake's schedule, `<` against `<=`, which differ only at the very instant a frame is due;
    - the arithmetic of the suite's too-long exposure, whose every mutant is an exposure out of range, refused the same;
    - the suite's snap at 1 ms made 1 s, an exposure the camera takes too.
