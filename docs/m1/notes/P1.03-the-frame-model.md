# P1.03: The frame model

- **Status:** In review
- **Branch:** `m1/p1.03-the-frame-model`
- **Spec:** [phases.md](../phases.md#p103-the-frame-model)
- **Size:** estimated 1,815 (code 860 · tests 800 · examples 110 · docs-as-code 25 · glue 20) → **measured 1,741** (code 988 · tests 630 · examples 95 · docs-as-code 4 · glue 24), **×0.96**. See [Size](#size).

## Summary

One way to hold a frame from any camera, real or virtual, shared without copying. A fixed pool of buffers lends frames to fill, shares them once frozen, and takes each buffer back when its last share drops. A running camera allocates nothing. Bayer patterns follow a crop, so colours can't be mislabelled. The frame bus's design is decided too, in ADR-0008, for P1.14a to build.

## What was built

In `tetera-core`'s new `frame` module:

- **`format`:**
  - `Channel` and `BayerPattern` (RGGB, GRBG, GBRG, BGGR), with `channel_at` and `cropped`;
  - `PixelFormat` (Bayer8, Bayer16 with its significant bits, Mono8, Mono16, Rgb8, Rgb16, Rgba8, PlanarF32), with sample sizes, planes, bits, and its pattern after a crop;
  - `FrameLayout`, with rows aligned to 64 bytes, `cropped` and `binned`;
  - `Binning`, 1 to 4 each way, a Bayer mosaic binning to an even size;
  - `to_full_range` and `from_full_range`.
- **`meta`:**
  - `FrameMeta`: sequence, the first row's exposure start and end, a rolling shutter's line time, exposure, gain, mode, binning, ROI, black level, objective, light, stage position, frames dropped;
  - `midpoint` and `row_midpoint`;
  - `LightState`.
- **`pool`:**
  - the `Allocator` and `BufferStorage` traits, and `HostAllocator`, whose buffers start on 4 KiB boundaries without unsafe code;
  - `FramePool` with `take` (waiting up to a timeout), `try_take` and `stats`;
  - `FrameMut`, with `freeze`, and `Frame`, cheap to clone.
- **`view`:**
  - `PixelView` for both frame types: planes, rows without padding, 16- and 8-bit samples, Bayer sites;
  - on `FrameMut`: `row_mut`, `set_sample_u16`, `fill_row_u16`.
- **`units`** gains `ms` and `ms_opt`, serde for durations as milliseconds. `duration_ms` now divides whole nanoseconds, so 12.5 ms reads 12.5, not 12.500000000000002.
- **The `frame_pool` example, and ADR-0008.**

## Ideas taken from OScope

From its documents, in our own words (ADR-0002):
- a pooled frame shared by reference count, with its line time and its stage position at the exposure's midpoint (OScope's plan, §5.5);
- the frame bus's two kinds of subscriber, lossless and latest-wins (the same section).

## Design notes and deviations from the plan

- **Rows align to 64 bytes** (`ROW_ALIGN`). A full MIChrome 20 row is 10,944 bytes, already a multiple, so the frame is exactly 39,923,712 bytes.
- **`Mono16` carries no significant bits;** only `Bayer16` does, as the spec lists.
- **A frame's buffer keeps the last frame's bytes** when lent again; zeroing 40 MB per frame would cost the camera's time.
- **`FrameMeta`'s exposure start and end are the first row's.** `row_midpoint` adds a line time per row for a rolling shutter.
- **The views copy samples out of little-endian bytes** rather than reinterpret memory: no unsafe code in L0. P1.05's kernels will read whole rows.
- **The frame bus is designed, not built:** ADR-0008 records it, and P1.14a builds it.

## Tests, and the mutants

- **92 tests in `tetera-core`** (35 new in the frame module, 2 in `units`):
  - **property tests:** layouts for random formats and sizes; every site's colour after a random crop;
  - **exhaustive:** every value of every bit depth from 1 to 16, scaled up and back;
  - **the pool:**
    - 1,000 take-and-share cycles on a pool of 4, with 4 allocations;
    - a buffer returning only with its last share;
    - an exhausted pool waiting its timeout, then failing with its message;
    - a waiting take getting the first buffer back;
    - a frame outliving its pool;
    - 8 threads × 500 frames through 4 buffers, losing none;
  - **alignment and zeroing** of buffers up to a full frame; the largest coordinates without overflow.
- **Broken on purpose:** returning a buffer to the pool, and the exhaustion timeout, as the spec asks. Their mutants (`give_back` emptied, the wait's deadline, the `misses` count) are caught.
- **Mutants on the frame module and `units`:**
  - the first run tested 317 and **missed 9**:
    - the `% 2` that keeps Bayer arithmetic from overflowing at the largest coordinates;
    - a buffer's exact slack;
    - `FrameMut::meta` never read;
    - `set_sample_u16` never tried on 8-bit and RGB16 frames;
  - tests were added for each;
  - now: **318 tested, 287 caught, 31 unviable, none missed.**
