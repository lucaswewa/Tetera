## Behaviour spec

Written before the code, as a spec-first phase's must be.

### What a PSF is

An objective images a point of light as a spot, its **point spread function** (PSF). A widefield image is the scene convolved with it, plus noise. Deconvolution estimates the scene from the image and the PSF.

### The theoretical widefield PSF

For an aberration-free objective of numerical aperture NA, at wavelength λ, in the paraxial scalar approximation (M. Born and E. Wolf, *Principles of Optics*, §8.5 and §8.8):

- **Through focus,** at lateral distance r and axial distance z from focus, with the optical coordinates
  - `v = 2π NA r / λ`,
  - `u = 2π NA² z / (n λ)`, n the refractive index of the medium, 1 for Tetera's dry objectives,

  the intensity is

  `I(u, v) = | 2 ∫₀¹ J₀(v ρ) exp(−i u ρ² / 2) ρ dρ |²`,

  over the pupil's radius ρ. Tetera integrates it numerically, by Simpson's rule over 400 intervals of ρ, enough for the rings out to v ≈ 40.
- **In focus,** u = 0, this is the **Airy pattern**, `I(v) = [2 J₁(v) / v]²`, which Tetera computes in closed form. The 3D PSF's focal plane matches it, to the integration's accuracy.
- **Bessel functions:** J₀ and J₁ by their power series for small arguments and their asymptotic expansions for large, each to about 1e-8 (Abramowitz and Stegun, 9.4.1–9.4.6), without a dependency.
- **Sampling:** at the camera's pixel pitch projected to the sample, in µm, and at a z step for 3D. Each pixel averages 5 × 5 points within it, since the Airy core spans only a few pixels at high magnification.
- **Its extent:** a square of odd size, centred on its middle pixel, wide enough for the Airy pattern's fourth dark ring. That ring lies at v ≈ 13.32, r = 13.32 λ / (2π NA). It is capped at 127 pixels, and a 3D PSF's planes cover the requested z range.
- **Normalised:** its values sum to 1, the whole stack for 3D. Convolving with it then keeps an image's total flux.

### A measured PSF

A bead's image, measured in M5, is loaded from a NumPy `.npy` file: little-endian 32- or 64-bit floats, 2D (y, x) or 3D (z, y, x), in C order. The format is NumPy's own documented format, and simple to write from Python. Tetera:
- checks every value is finite and none negative;
- takes the file as centred, its middle pixel on the bead;
- normalises it to sum 1.

### Richardson–Lucy

W. H. Richardson ("Bayesian-based iterative method of image restoration", JOSA 1972) and L. B. Lucy ("An iterative technique for the rectification of observed distributions", AJ 1974) derived the same iteration. It is the maximum-likelihood estimate for Poisson noise, starting from the observed image d:

`u₀ = d`, and `u_{k+1} = u_k · [ (d / (u_k ⊛ h)) ⊛ h* ]`,

h the PSF and h* the PSF mirrored. Tetera's choices:
- **Convolutions by FFT:** the PSF padded to the image's size, its centre moved to the origin, transformed once (its OTF). Each iteration then takes two forward and two inverse transforms. FFTs are `rustfft`'s, in single precision, as cuFFT's will be (P1.07c).
- **Boundaries:** the image is padded by the PSF's half-size on every side, mirrored without repeating the edge, so the FFT's wrap-around falls in the padding; the result is cropped back. Without padding, an edge's light would leak to the opposite edge.
- **Division:** where `u_k ⊛ h` is below 1e-12 of the image's largest value, the ratio is taken as 1, so an empty region stays empty rather than divide by zero.
- **Non-negative:** an estimate starting non-negative stays so; values below zero from rounding are clamped to zero.
- **Flux:** with a normalised PSF, under circular convolution, each iteration keeps the total `Σ u = Σ d`, to rounding. Over the padded domain that holds; cropping to the image keeps it to within what the padding held.
- **Total-variation regularisation** (N. Dey and others, "Richardson–Lucy algorithm with total variation regularization for 3D confocal microscope deconvolution", Microsc. Res. Tech. 2006), optional:

  `u_{k+1} = u_k · [ (d / (u_k ⊛ h)) ⊛ h* ] / (1 − λ div(∇u_k / |∇u_k|))`.

  It uses forward differences for the gradient, backward ones for the divergence, and |∇u| at least 1e-6 of the image's largest value. λ is small, about 0.002. Where the denominator would fall below 0.1, it is held at 0.1, so a large λ can't flip a pixel's sign.
- **Iterations:** set by the caller; no stopping rule. RL's error against the truth falls, then rises as it fits the noise. The example shows where.
- **2D and 3D** by the same code: an N-dimensional FFT over each axis in turn.

### The backends' tolerance (for P1.07c)

The GPU will compute the same iteration with cuFFT in single precision. FFTs on two libraries round differently, and the iteration compounds it, so the twins can't agree exactly. After 20 iterations on a 12-bit image, the relative RMS difference between the CPU and the GPU must be below 1e-3. Every pixel must be within 1 % of the image's largest value.

### What the tests check

- **PSFs:**
  - J₀ and J₁ against tabulated values;
  - the Airy pattern's zeros where J₁ has them;
  - normalisation to 1, and symmetry in x, y and across the diagonal;
  - the 3D PSF's focal plane against the Airy pattern;
  - its planes symmetric in z;
  - a wider NA giving a narrower PSF;
  - the NPY loader on files it writes, and on bad ones.
- **Richardson–Lucy:**
  - **flux:** conserved over its domain;
  - **convergence:** a point source, blurred, sharpens towards a point, its peak rising each iteration;
  - **the error:** RL lowers it against the ground truth on a blurred, noisy synthetic image. The numbers are recorded;
  - a flat image unchanged;
  - 3D as well as 2D;
  - total variation smoothing noise in a flat region.
- **Broken on purpose:**
  - **flux:** the PSF left unnormalised makes the flux drift, which its test catches;
  - **normalisation:** the PSF's own normalisation removed fails its test.
