## Behaviour spec

Written before the code, as a spec-first phase's must be.

### What a PSF is

An objective images a point of light as a spot, its **point spread function** (PSF). A widefield image is the scene convolved with it, plus noise. Deconvolution estimates the scene from the image and the PSF.

### The theoretical widefield PSF

For an aberration-free objective of numerical aperture NA, at wavelength λ, in the paraxial scalar approximation (M. Born and E. Wolf, *Principles of Optics*, §8.5 and §8.8):

- **Through focus,** at lateral distance r and axial distance z from focus, with the optical coordinates
  - `v = 2π NA r / λ`,
  - `u = 2π NA² z / (n λ)`, n the refractive index of the medium, 1 for Tetera's dry objectives,

  the intensity is

  `I(u, v) = | 2 ∫₀¹ J₀(v ρ) exp(−i u ρ² / 2) ρ dρ |²`,

  over the pupil's radius ρ. Tetera integrates it numerically, by Simpson's rule over 400 intervals of ρ, enough for the rings out to v ≈ 40.
- **In focus,** u = 0, this is the **Airy pattern**, `I(v) = [2 J₁(v) / v]²`, which Tetera computes in closed form. The 3D PSF's focal plane matches it, to the integration's accuracy.
- **Bessel functions:** J₀ and J₁ by their power series for small arguments and their asymptotic expansions for large, each to about 1e-8 (Abramowitz and Stegun, 9.4.1–9.4.6), without a dependency.
- **Sampling:** at the camera's pixel pitch projected to the sample, in µm, and at a z step for 3D. Each pixel averages 5 × 5 points within it, since the Airy core spans only a few pixels at high magnification.
- **Its extent:** a square of odd size, centred on its middle pixel, wide enough for the Airy pattern's fourth dark ring. That ring lies at v ≈ 13.32, r = 13.32 λ / (2π NA). It is capped at 127 pixels, and a 3D PSF's planes cover the requested z range.
- **Normalised:** its values sum to 1, the whole stack for 3D. Convolving with it then keeps an image's total flux.

### A measured PSF

A bead's image, measured in M5, is loaded from a NumPy `.npy` file: little-endian 32- or 64-bit floats, 2D (y, x) or 3D (z, y, x), in C order. The format is NumPy's own documented format, and simple to write from Python. Tetera:
- checks every value is finite and none negative;
- takes the file as centred, its middle pixel on the bead;
- normalises it to sum 1.

### Richardson–Lucy

W. H. Richardson ("Bayesian-based iterative method of image restoration", JOSA 1972) and L. B. Lucy ("An iterative technique for the rectification of observed distributions", AJ 1974) derived the same iteration. It is the maximum-likelihood estimate for Poisson noise, starting from the observed image d:

`u₀ = d`, and `u_{k+1} = u_k · [ (d / (u_k ⊛ h)) ⊛ h* ]`,

h the PSF and h* the PSF mirrored. Tetera's choices:
- **Convolutions by FFT:** the PSF padded to the image's size, its centre moved to the origin, transformed once (its OTF). Each iteration then takes two forward and two inverse transforms. FFTs are `rustfft`'s, in single precision, as cuFFT's will be (P1.07c).
- **Boundaries:** the image is padded by the PSF's half-size on every side, mirrored without repeating the edge, so the FFT's wrap-around falls in the padding; the result is cropped back. Without padding, an edge's light would leak to the opposite edge.
- **Division:** where `u_k ⊛ h` is below 1e-12 of the image's largest value, the ratio is taken as 1, so an empty region stays empty rather than divide by zero.
- **Non-negative:** an estimate starting non-negative stays so; values below zero from rounding are clamped to zero.
- **Flux:** with a normalised PSF, under circular convolution, each iteration keeps the total `Σ u = Σ d`, to rounding. Over the padded domain that holds; cropping to the image keeps it to within what the padding held.
- **Total-variation regularisation** (N. Dey and others, "Richardson–Lucy algorithm with total variation regularization for 3D confocal microscope deconvolution", Microsc. Res. Tech. 2006), optional:

  `u_{k+1} = u_k · [ (d / (u_k ⊛ h)) ⊛ h* ] / (1 − λ div(∇u_k / |∇u_k|))`.

  It uses forward differences for the gradient, backward ones for the divergence, and |∇u| at least 1e-6 of the image's largest value. λ is small, about 0.002. Where the denominator would fall below 0.1, it is held at 0.1, so a large λ can't flip a pixel's sign.
- **Iterations:** set by the caller; no stopping rule. RL's error against the truth falls, then rises as it fits the noise. The example shows where.
- **2D and 3D** by the same code: an N-dimensional FFT over each axis in turn.

### The backends' tolerance (for P1.07c)

The GPU will compute the same iteration with cuFFT in single precision. FFTs on two libraries round differently, and the iteration compounds it, so the twins can't agree exactly. After 20 iterations on a 12-bit image, the relative RMS difference between the CPU and the GPU must be below 1e-3. Every pixel must be within 1 % of the image's largest value.

### What the tests check

- **PSFs:**
  - J₀ and J₁ against tabulated values;
  - the Airy pattern's zeros where J₁ has them;
  - normalisation to 1, and symmetry in x, y and across the diagonal;
  - the 3D PSF's focal plane against the Airy pattern;
  - its planes symmetric in z;
  - a wider NA giving a narrower PSF;
  - the NPY loader on files it writes, and on bad ones.
- **Richardson–Lucy:**
  - **flux:** conserved over its domain;
  - **convergence:** a point source, blurred, sharpens towards a point, its peak rising each iteration;
  - **the error:** RL lowers it against the ground truth on a blurred, noisy synthetic image. The numbers are recorded;
  - a flat image unchanged;
  - 3D as well as 2D;
  - total variation smoothing noise in a flat region.
- **Broken on purpose:**
  - **flux:** the PSF left unnormalised makes the flux drift, which its test catches;
  - **normalisation:** the PSF's own normalisation removed fails its test.

## Summary

Tetera can now deconvolve, on the CPU, in 2D and 3D.
- **PSFs:** the widefield PSF from the objective's NA, the wavelength and the pixel size, in focus and through it, normalised and centred; or a measured one from a NumPy file.
- **Richardson–Lucy** keeps the flux over its domain: it drifted by 3.4e-8 in 10 iterations. A blurred point gathers its light back each iteration, from 411 of its 4,000 in its centre pixel to 2,397 after 15.
- **The error against the truth falls,** as the phase's *Done when* asks:
  - on the tests' 64 × 64 specimen, blurred with noise of σ 20, the RMS error went from **192.3 to 143.0** after 20 iterations;
  - on the example's 1,024 × 1,024 specimen, with noise of σ 15, it fell from **63.2 to 59.0** at iteration 28, then rose as Richardson–Lucy fitted the noise;
  - with total variation at λ 0.002, it fell to **57.9** at iteration 52.

The behaviour spec above was committed before the code, in `d513b19`. Q21's answer stands: 2D and 3D, a theoretical PSF now and a measured one in M5.

## Performance

From `deconvolve` in release on 16 threads:

| | Size | Padded | An iteration |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2D | 1,024 × 1,024, a 41 × 41 PSF | 1,080 × 1,080 | 28 ms |
| 3D | 192 × 192 × 48, a 41 × 41 × 15 PSF | 240 × 240 × 63 | 98 ms |

**Threads:** a 1,024 × 1,024 iteration took 33 ms on one thread, 15 ms on four and 23 ms on sixteen. The columns gathered for the y axis are bound by memory, not arithmetic, so more threads don't help. The CPU is the fallback. The GPU twin is P1.07c's, and P1.07c times 20 iterations at 20 MP.

**At 20 MP,** scaling those numbers, an iteration would take about half a second on the CPU.

## Design notes and deviations from the plan

- **Richardson–Lucy doesn't care how the PSF is scaled.** The spec expected a PSF left unnormalised to make the flux drift. It doesn't: a scale S divides the ratio d / (u ⊛ Sh) and multiplies the correction (·) ⊛ Sh* by the same S, which cancel. An unnormalised PSF fails the PSF's own tests, and the convolution's flux. So the flux was broken on purpose another way, by leaving out the correlation step; see below.
- **The padding:** the spec pads by the PSF's half-size. Tetera pads by at least that, up to the next size whose only prime factors are 2, 3, 5 and 7, which FFTs transform fastest, cuFFT's among them. The extra padding continues the mirror. When the PSF is wider than the image, the reflection repeats as often as it takes.
- **The PSF through focus:** each plane's radial profile is integrated once, on a grid of a twentieth of a pixel, and interpolated linearly between. Integrating at each of a pixel's 25 points would take 25 times as long, for a difference far below the PSF's own accuracy. In focus, it uses the closed form.
- **2D and 3D together:** a 2D PSF on a volume deconvolves each plane alone, by the same code. A 3D PSF on a single image is refused: the image holds nothing from the other planes to recover.
- **Complex FFTs throughout,** not real to complex: twice the work, but the GPU twin will use cuFFT's complex transforms (P1.07c) and match them step by step. Real FFTs are a possible later speed-up for both.
- **Unnormalised FFTs:** neither rustfft nor cuFFT scales its inverse, so the PSF's transform is divided once by the padded count instead.
- **A least grain for each parallel loop,** 32 Ki values: under WSL, waking rayon's threads costs about half a millisecond. Before it, an iteration on 128 × 128 took 17 ms on 16 threads against 0.4 ms on one; after, 0.5 ms.
- **The NumPy reader** finds the header's three keys by name rather than parsing Python. It reads versions 1 to 3 of the format, and writes version 1, padded to 64 bytes as NumPy does.
- **The PSF's extent** is capped at 127 pixels, a half-width of 63, as the spec says.

## Tests, and the mutants

- **23 new tests,** all in every build. `tetera-imaging` now has 101 without the feature.
  - **In `psf`, 11:**
    - J₀ and J₁ against tables at six points, their parity, and their first zeros;
    - the Airy pattern's centre, its half-maximum at v = 1.616, and its zeros at J₁'s;
    - the through-focus integral equal to the Airy pattern in focus, and to its closed form on the axis, `[sin(u/4) / (u/4)]²`;
    - its amplitude, real and imaginary parts, against their closed forms on the axis and in focus;
    - a plane's radial profile interpolating the integral between its samples;
    - **a fine PSF's values** against the Airy pattern at 14 offsets, and its centre falling through focus as `[sin(u/4) / (u/4)]²` says, each within 1 %;
    - normalisation, centring, and symmetry in x, y and across the diagonal;
    - the extent to the fourth dark ring, and its cap;
    - a wider NA giving a narrower PSF;
    - the 3D PSF's focal plane against the Airy pattern, its planes symmetric through focus and dimming away from it;
    - NumPy files round-tripped in 2D, 3D, f4 and f8, and in format version 2, and bad ones refused, one reason each.
  - **In `deconvolve`, 12:**
    - the reflection and the padded sizes;
    - the transform against a plain DFT in 3D, and back;
    - a point convolved to the PSF, a flat image kept flat, a ramp kept at an edge;
    - **the flux** kept to 1e-6 at every iteration;
    - **a point sharpening** at every iteration;
    - **the error against the truth** falling by more than a fifth;
    - a flat image unchanged, an empty one left empty, its correction finite;
    - **3D:** a point sharpened in every axis, the flux kept;
    - **total variation:** its value on a flat volume and at a point, worked by hand, and held at 0.1; and smoothing noise in a flat region, to 19.6 with λ 0.01 against 47.4 plain, keeping the region's level within 1 %;
    - bad volumes and settings refused.
- **The first run found** J₁ with the wrong sign above 3: `copysign` gave it x's sign rather than keeping its own, which J₁(5) = −0.328 caught. The Airy pattern squares J₁, so nothing else had shown it.
- **Broken on purpose,** as the spec asks:
  - **flux:** Richardson–Lucy without its correlation step, the ratio used as the correction. It failed three tests: the flux, which drifted by 2.4e-3 in the first iteration; the error against the truth; and 3D;
  - **normalisation:** the PSF's normalisation removed. It failed eight tests: the PSF's normalisation, the 3D PSF, the wider NA, the refusals and the NumPy files, which check normalised values; and the convolution's flux, and the two deconvolutions whose test images it blurs.
- **Mutants,** over this phase's changes, without the feature: the phase adds no CUDA code.
  - **The first run:** 674 tested, 617 caught, 35 missed, 17 unviable, and 5 timeouts, where a mutant loops for ever in `smooth` or the NumPy header's padding. A timeout counts as caught.
  - **What it missed, and what changed:**
    - **the PSF's values,** which no test had checked against theory. The optical coordinates u and v, the planes' depths and the profile's interpolation could all change unseen, since a wrong PSF is still normalised, centred and symmetric. Now tested against the Airy pattern, the fall through focus, and the profile itself;
    - **the pupil integral's signs,** which squaring hides: now tested through the amplitude itself;
    - **the NumPy header's lengths:** version 2, and files cut short;
    - **the ratio's guard:** `>=` for `>` gives 0 / 0 on an empty image, which the clamp then hid. Now the correction is checked finite;
    - **code no test could tell apart, rewritten:**
      - the Airy pattern's guard, now at 0 alone, where the ratio is exact elsewhere;
      - J₁'s sign, now by `is_sign_negative`;
      - the mean over a pixel's 25 points, which normalisation makes the sum serve for;
      - the profile's index clamp, which never bound;
    - **the parallel grain,** which only changes speed: now one helper, `per_task`, its mutants excluded with that reason.
  - **The second run:** 663 tested, 640 caught, 1 missed, 17 unviable, 5 timeouts. The one missed, total variation's division as `%`, made every value collapse below 1, which the noise test read as smoothing. That test now also checks the region's level is kept, which catches it.

## Follow-ups and known gaps

- **Richardson–Lucy on the GPU, with cuFFT:** P1.07c, within the tolerance this spec states. Its break on purpose, the correction step's mirrored PSF, needs an asymmetric PSF: a widefield PSF is its own mirror image.
- **Measured PSFs from beads:** M5.
- **Faster CPU transforms,** blocked transposes and real FFTs, if CPU deconvolution ever matters: an extension.
