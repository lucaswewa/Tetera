## Summary

Tetera has a stage that moves the way a grblHAL one does, in time that tests control. The phase's *Done when* holds:
- **`VirtualStage` passes the stage contract suite:** at 1×, at 10×, and starting in alarm.
- **A move's duration matches the trapezoid's within 1 ms in stepped time,** for three moves, against the formula worked out in the test.
- **The same steps give the same track, bit for bit:** a run of moves, jogs, holds, homing and a hard limit, through all six states, twice.

What it does:
- **trapezoidal moves,** within each axis's limits;
- **grblHAL's six states;**
- **soft limits,** and homing that takes time;
- **feed holds and jog cancels;**
- **reports** at a set rate, each arriving late, kept in a track;
- **faults on demand:** an alarm, a hard limit, a stall.

## What was built

- **In `tetera-sim`,** `stage`:
  - `VirtualStageConfig`, checked, with `VirtualAxis` (travel and limits): 100 × 75 mm in x and y at 20 mm/s and 200 mm/s², 10 mm in z at 2 mm/s and 20 mm/s², reports every 20 ms arriving 5 ms late, by default;
  - `GrblState`: Idle, Jog, Run, Hold, Home and Alarm, and `hal`, the HAL's state for each;
  - `Report`, and the track;
  - `VirtualStageFault`: an alarm, a hard limit, a stall;
  - `VirtualStage`: the `Stage` trait, with `grbl_state`, `position` (where the stage itself is, ahead of its reports), `track`, `feed_hold` and `inject`.
- **In `tetera-core`:** `Timestamp::saturating_add`, as `UtcTime` has.
- **The `virtual-stage` example.**
- **No new dependencies;** `tetera-sim` now uses `tetera-hal`, which the layering allows, and `serde_json` for its example.

## Design notes and deviations from the plan

- **A plan of legs:**
  - each command replaces what the stage does from then on with legs, each part of a motion profile in one state, and a state once they end;
  - position and state at any time come from the plan, from the time alone;
  - a feed hold or a jog cancel replaces the leg under way with the same profile slowing to rest (P1.10a's `hold`);
  - a stall cuts the leg at half its time, which is half its length, the trapezoid being symmetric.
- **Reports from the plan:**
  - report k is taken at k report periods on the clock, and arrives a latency later;
  - before a command changes the plan, every report taken by then is taken from the old one, and held until it arrives;
  - so a report never shows a command it came before, and a report taken at the very time of a command came before it.
- **`wait` wants a report taken after the last command, showing the stage still,** as a real grblHAL driver must. Otherwise a wait straight after a command would see the report from before it, still idle. The new test of a wait on a clock nobody steps found exactly that, at a command on the very time of a report.
- **`stop` and `stop_jog` wait for rest,** as the HAL asks. On a stepped clock that means another thread must step it, so `feed_hold` holds without waiting, and each wait has a limit in the host's time.
- **Events come from reports,** as they arrive: an error before the alarm it causes, and the HAL's state as it changes. A thread of the stage's own catches up as each report is due, so events come without anyone asking; it holds the stage weakly, and ends when the stage is dropped.
- **A hold is a move's state while it slows;** a jog cancel stays a jog. The HAL sees both as moving.
- **Stopping homing is an alarm,** as on grblHAL.
- **The stall is one grblHAL would notice,** as with a stepper driver's stall detection: it stops and raises an alarm. A stall it wouldn't notice, steps lost silently, is left for when the virtual camera can show it (P1.12).
- **Moves don't queue,** as in the HAL (ADR-0013): a move while moving is `Busy`.

## Tests, and the mutants

- **31 tests in the crate, 16 of them new:**
  - the contract suite at 1×, 10× and from an alarm;
  - a move's duration in stepped time against the trapezoid, within 1 ms;
  - the same run twice, bit for bit, through all six states;
  - reports at their rate, arriving late, the stage ahead of them;
  - a report taken before a command, or at its very time, showing the stage before it;
  - a feed hold slowing to rest in the hold state, within its slowing time and distance;
  - a jog to each axis's own end, or cancelled, slowing as a jog;
  - homing: refused moves in alarm, z first, then x and y, then locating, in the time the trapezoids and the locate take;
  - busy while moving; a wait no longer than asked; a stop while homing, an alarm; stopping a still stage;
  - each fault, its error and its alarm; a stall halfway, once;
  - events without being asked;
  - the track's length;
  - a move by from where the stage itself is;
  - each thing wrong with a configuration;
  - grblHAL's states as the HAL knows them;
  - dropping the stage letting its thread go.
- **Broken on purpose,** as the spec asks:
  - **a soft limit:** without it, the contract suite failed with "X to 100000.00000000001 µm, beyond its travel";
  - **a state transition,** a move let through in alarm: the homing test failed, the move accepted.
- **Run 15 times,** the tests passed each time.
- **Mutants,** of the stage and `Timestamp::saturating_add`: 109 in the phase's diff; **61 caught, 34 unviable, 14 timed out, none missed.**
  - **Timed out:** each breaks the reports (when they are taken, when they arrive, or how they join the track) or a wait, so a wait never ends, and the tests hang until the time limit.
  - **Runs 1 to 3 missed 8, then 2:**
    - **Untested:** a jog's travel on y and z, and the configuration as made. Each has a check now.
    - **Redundant:** the plan's end, which `wait` didn't need, going report by report; and a guard on a wait's time limit. Both are gone.
    - **Two edges:** a wait's due time made zero, which spun forever on a clock nobody steps; and a report taken at the very time of a command. Their tests found the bug in `wait` above.
  - **Two exclusions, with their reasons in `.cargo/mutants.toml`:**
    - `<` against `<=` where the plan's legs meet;
    - the jog's direction at a speed of zero, which is refused first.
