## Summary

The H&E sample is made on the GPU as fast as the camera needs it. The phase's *Done when* holds:
- **The same seed gives the same tile bits on the GPU,** and a seed one bit different changes most of them.
- **The backends agree within tolerance,** on six regions: whole sections at 2, 1, 0.48 and 0.048 µm, slices, and a fold.
  - The spec allows 2e-3 OD plus 1e-3 of the value, and no value beyond 0.05.
  - The worst difference was 6.0e-4 OD, and no value went beyond the first bound. Most of that is half precision's rounding.
- **A 512 × 512 region at 50× takes 1.87 ms on the GPU,** both passes, measured by the GPU's own events.
- **The PNGs, the GPU's beside the CPU's,** are written for the owner to judge at Checkpoint A.

What it adds:
- **the model in CUDA,** a port of the CPU twin in its order of operations;
- **half precision** on the GPU;
- **tiles,** keyed by level of detail, place and depth, in an LRU cache within a VRAM budget.

## What was built

- **In `tetera-compute`:**
  - **`kernels/he.cu`:** the noise, the section and its tissue, and rendering, ported from `tetera-sim`. `he_nuclei` and `he_red_cells` decide each grid cell's nucleus or red cell at its own centre, one thread a cell. `he_render` makes each pixel, one thread a pixel, and writes its two stains as halves;
  - **`he`:** `HeParams`, `HeRegion`, `grid` (the cells a region's nuclei come from, as the CPU twin takes them), and `CudaBackend::he_render`, which launches the three kernels;
  - **`half`:** IEEE half precision to and from single, rounding to nearest, ties to even, as PTX's `cvt.rn.f16.f32` does.
- **In `tetera-sim`:**
  - **`tiles`:** `TileKey` (level, column, row, `TileDepth`), with its region and `level_for` a sampling; and `TileCache`, an LRU cache within a budget of bytes, whatever a tile holds, with its `CacheStats`;
  - **`gpu`,** with the `cuda` feature:
    - `HeSample::render_on`, the stains left on the GPU, and `render_gpu`, brought back;
    - `HeTiles`, the sample's tiles on the GPU, each holding a grant from a VRAM budget until it goes;
    - `TILE_BYTES`, 256 KiB.
- **The `he-sample` example,** with `--features cuda`: each view on the GPU too, timed and compared with the CPU, as `-gpu.png`; and a field of tiles moving across the slide through a cache.
- **No new dependencies.**

## Performance

- **The GPU is 9 to 19 times as fast as the CPU twin on 8 cores,** more at coarse samplings, where each pixel averages more points.
- **A 512 × 512 region at 50×:** 1.87 ms on the GPU, cells and pixels both; the CPU takes about 17 ms.
- **A camera frame:** a full 20 MP frame at 50× would take about 150 ms rendered whole. The live view instead takes tiles from the cache (P1.12, P1.14), and makes only those that move into view.

## Design notes and deviations from the plan

- **The kernels live in `tetera-compute`,** the one crate allowed unsafe code, which a launch needs. `tetera-sim` holds the model and its CPU twin, and passes the kernels plain numbers: the folded seed, the section, the region.
- **The same two passes as the CPU,** in the same order of operations:
  - the hash, the lattice split and each grid cell's choices are exactly the same on both backends, so no nucleus appears on one and not the other;
  - the rest differs in its last bits, from CUDA's own `sinf`, `cosf` and `atan2f`, and the fused multiply-adds NVRTC makes, which Rust doesn't. Half precision dominates the difference.
- **Half precision without a header:** the kernel rounds with PTX's own `cvt.rn.f16.f32`, which NVRTC needs no include for. `tetera-compute`'s `half` module reads the bits on the host, and rounds the same way, tested against every one of the 65,536 halves.
- **Tiles meet without seams:** four tiles side by side match the region they make on the CPU, within the tolerance. Their pixels' positions come from each tile's own corner, so they differ from a whole region's in their last bits, not more.
- **The cache doesn't care what a tile holds,** so its LRU logic is tested without a GPU. On the GPU each tile holds a grant from a `VramBudget`, returned when the tile goes. A tile made beyond the budget is refused, as the break on purpose showed.
- **A tie in the LRU,** which can't happen, goes to the least key, so a test can't pass by luck if the use count ever stops counting.
- **The tolerance's first bound,** 2e-3 OD plus 1e-3 of the value, wasn't reached by any value; the second, 0.05 OD, guards against a nucleus decided differently.

## Tests, and the mutants

- **14 new tests:** 5 in `tetera-compute` and 9 in `tetera-sim`, 63 there now. The 5 GPU tests run with `TETERA_CUDA=1`.
  - **half precision:** known halves read as their values; floats rounded to the nearest, ties to even, subnormals and infinity among them; every half through a round trip; within 1 part in 2,000;
  - **the kernels' grid** of cells, off zero in both directions;
  - **tiles:** a tile's region at its level; a sampling's level; a budget holding one tile at least; the least recently used going first; a tile that fails to be made not kept;
  - **on the GPU:**
    - the twins within tolerance on six regions;
    - the same seed's bits, and a seed one bit different;
    - four tiles meeting their region without seams, the cache within its budget, each grant returned;
    - a tile's 256 KiB;
    - a 512 × 512 region's time.
- **Broken on purpose,** as the spec asks: **the cache's budget,** a tile kept beyond it. Two tests caught it: the LRU's ("the least recent went"), and on the GPU the VRAM budget itself, which refused the extra tile.
- **compute-sanitizer:** the GPU tests run under memcheck with 0 errors and 0 bytes leaked. With a buffer made too small on purpose, it reported "Invalid __global__ write".
- **Run 15 times,** the GPU tests passed each time.
- **Mutants,** of both crates with CUDA on and the GPU tests enabled: 262 in the phase's diff; the last runs missed none.
  - **The first run missed 28:**
    - the twins and tiles were compared without checking their lengths, so an empty result passed;
    - the tile's size wasn't pinned;
    - the grid test's numbers hid its arithmetic, at zero, twice over;
    - the LRU could pass by luck in a tie;
    - a NaN half's payload, now fixed, as a stain's NaN carries nothing.
  - **Three exclusions, with their reasons in `.cargo/mutants.toml`:**
    - `|` against `^`, where the fields of a half share no bit;
    - `<` against `<=` at the smallest normal half, which both paths encode the same;
    - **the kernels' buffer sizes:** one made too small is written out of bounds, which no test can see. compute-sanitizer can, and the real launches run clean under it.
