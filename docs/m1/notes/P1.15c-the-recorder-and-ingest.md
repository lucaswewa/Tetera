- **Status:** Done
- **Branch:** `m1/p1.15c-the-recorder-and-ingest`

## Summary

Every frame of a capture goes onto the scratch disk at the camera's full rate, then into a dataset. The recorder, a lossless subscriber of the frame bus, writes raw frames to a frame log of preallocated segments; ingest, run as a compute job, writes the log into a P1.15b dataset. The phase's *Done when* holds:

- **The recorder keeps up with the virtual camera at its full rate, 289 MB/s, on ext4, without a dropped frame:** at 5472 × 3648, 12 bits in 16, 7.24 frames a second, it recorded 28 of 28 frames in 4 s, none missing, the camera never held, in four runs of five. The fifth, straight after a build and a delete of 1.3 GB, found the system disk too slow for long enough to fill the recorder's buffer: the camera was held and dropped frames, though the recorder lost none (see Performance).
- **Ingest writes a dataset that reads back exactly:** the dataset's first frame equals the log's, sample for sample; in the tests, every frame and its metadata.

## What was built

- **In `tetera-store`:**
  - **the frame log:** `FrameLogWriter` appends raw frames, rows packed, to segment files preallocated `segment_frames` at a time, each cut to its frames when it closes, and a line for each frame in `index.jsonl`; `finish` puts it all on disk. `FrameLog` reads it back in order, passing over a crash's torn last line;
  - **`ingest`:** a frame log into a new dataset, calling a step before each frame and at the end; an error from the step stops it, and the half-written dataset goes with its writer;
  - **`DatasetWriter::append_packed`** and **`FrameLogWriter::append_packed`,** a frame as the log keeps it, and as the recorder copies it.
- **In `tetera-acquire`:**
  - **`Recorder`:** a lossless subscriber's frames into a frame log. One thread copies each frame out at once, so its buffer goes straight back to the camera's pool; another writes the copies, with 16 of them in memory between, 640 MB of full frames, to ride out the disk's stalls. Only when those are full does the bus hold the camera. `stop` writes what is queued and finishes the log; `RecordStats` counts the frames, bytes and rate, the times the camera was held, and any frames missing;
  - **the example `recorder`:** the camera at full rate onto disk, then ingested as a compute job.
- **No new dependencies.**

## Performance

- **WSL2's ext4 disk is fast, but not steady.** `dd` wrote 1.1 GB/s with a final `fdatasync`, and 2 to 3 GB/s direct. But buffered writes of 40 MB frames, sustained, ran from 240 to 490 MB/s from one minute to the next, with single writes stalling up to 0.8 s as writeback caught up, and slower still just after a build or a large delete.
- **So the recorder buffers.** Its first design wrote each frame from the camera's own buffer: with the camera's pool of four, one slow write left the camera without a buffer, and it dropped frames, 22 of 33 in one run, though the recorder lost none. Copying each frame out at once, about 10 ms for 40 MB, gives the buffer straight back, and 16 copies in memory ride out a stall of 2 s at the camera's rate.
- **Recording at the camera's full rate,** 289 MB/s, on an idle disk: 28 of 28 frames, 280 to 284 MB/s, none missing, the camera never held, in each run. Straight after a build and a delete of 1.3 GB, the disk fell well below 289 MB/s for long enough to fill the buffer: the camera was held three times and dropped 12 frames of 34. The recorder never loses a frame; a busy system disk can still fall behind the camera (Q14: a scratch disk of its own).
- **The camera's first frame after a mode change** builds the mode's buffers on the GPU, and the frames behind it are dropped: the example takes a snap before it records, as P1.13b's notes found.
- **Ingest** ran at 99 to 127 MB/s of raw frames, compressing them 1.7 : 1 with zstd on one thread, a chunk a frame. A capture of 28 full frames takes 9 s to ingest, in the background as a compute job.

## Design notes and deviations from the plan

- **Ingest lives in `tetera-store`,** below the job service: a step closure carries the job's progress and cancel check, so the store needn't know of jobs.
- **Segments are preallocated with `set_len`,** which on ext4 makes them sparse rather than reserving their blocks; the segments are cut to their frames when they close.
- **The index is written after each frame,** so every line names a frame that is there, and flushed when the log finishes.
- **The recorder counts missing frames** by gaps in their numbers and by the camera's own count of drops; frames lost after the last one recorded can't show in it.
- **Stopping writes only what is queued,** so a recording ends even while the camera streams on.
- **Two of P1.14b's tests were fixed here,** as the gate's stress step and the repeated runs found them flaky under load:
  - the slow client's: a fresh frame's age at sending reached 110 ms with three copies of the tests on two CPUs each, against a bound of 100 ms and a broken window's 134 ms. The client now paints in 300 ms, a stale frame would be 300 ms old, and the bound is 200 ms;
  - the client state's: frames that came while it read the errors went unacknowledged, so its last acknowledgement, of an older frame, left the window full. It now acknowledges every frame so far.

## Tests, and the mutants

- **5 new tests:**
  - **the frame log:** round trips across segments, for 16 and 8 bits, each segment cut to its frames; a torn last line passed over, a bad line refused; bad shapes, an existing folder and a frame that doesn't fit refused;
  - **ingest:** a log into a dataset, exactly, each step called; stopped half way and at the end, leaving no dataset; a packed frame of the wrong size refused;
  - **the recorder:** a slow recorder holding the camera and losing no frame; missing frames counted; a stop that ends while the camera streams; a frame of the wrong shape ending the recording in an error; bad options refused; a recorder dropped finishing its log;
  - **a frame appended packed,** in the store, as the recorder copies it.
- **Broken on purpose,** as the spec asks: **the recorder's backpressure,** a full queue dropping the frame instead of holding the camera: the slow recorder's test failed, "no frame lost", 3 frames recorded of 40.
- **Run 15 times,** the three crates' tests, `tetera-store`'s, `tetera-acquire`'s and `tetera-api`'s, 68 in all, passed each time, once P1.14b's client-state test was fixed (see the design notes); before that, one run in 15 failed on it.
- **Mutants:** 
  - **the first run,** over the change, 102 tested: 84 caught, 10 unviable, 8 missed: the rate's arithmetic; the packing path, which no test frame's padded rows reached; the segment's preallocated size, seen only after its cut; a whole but bad last line in the index; an empty log; and ingest taking its count from the log, which a spec asking for more than the log held hid.
  - **Tests now cover them,** with no exclusions: padded rows, the preallocated size, a bad last line, an empty log, the exact rate, and a spec asking for fewer frames than the log holds.
  - **After the recorder's buffering,** a run over it and the log's writer missed `append_packed`, which only the recorder's tests used; the store now tests it too.
  - **The last runs,** over the functions changed, 39 and 7 tested: **all caught**.

## Follow-ups and known gaps

- **Capture,** which records and ingests through the jobs, and the `capture` and `datasets` Things: P1.15d.
- **Ingest on more threads:** zstd on one thread is 127 MB/s; chunks could compress side by side.
- **Real preallocation** with `fallocate`, if a full disk mid-recording matters; it would need a system call the standard library doesn't offer.
- **Direct I/O** would avoid the page cache's stalls, at 2 to 3 GB/s here, but needs page-aligned buffers, which take `unsafe` code that `tetera-acquire` and `tetera-store` don't allow.
- **The scratch disk** (Q14): the frame log is on WSL2's ext4 disk here; a second disk would keep recordings off the system's.
