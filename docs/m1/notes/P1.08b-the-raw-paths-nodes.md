## Summary

The raw path is now a set of nodes, from a raw frame to a JPEG, and the live chain is a recipe.
- **Ten stock nodes:** `tetera.bin`, `tetera.bayer_split`, `tetera.superpixel`, `tetera.demosaic`, `tetera.dark`, `tetera.flat`, `tetera.resize`, `tetera.colour`, `tetera.display` and `tetera.jpeg`. Each wraps its `tetera-imaging` function, on whichever backend `Compute` chose, and matches it exactly.
- **[`recipes/live-chain.json`](../../../crates/tetera-pipeline/recipes/live-chain.json)** runs the live chain node by node: dark, flat, demosaic, resize, colour, JPEG. It gives **exactly the preview `Isp::run` gives**, on the CPU and on CUDA.
- **An encoded image** is now a port type, JPEG first, and [ADR-0012](../../adr/0012-the-pipeline-model.md) records it.
- **On a 20 MP raw frame,** the recipe takes 170 to 205 ms on the CPU and 215 to 245 ms on CUDA, where every node copies its data to the GPU and back. Keeping the data there, and fusing the chain, is P1.08c's.

## What was built

- **`stock`** in `tetera-pipeline`:
  - one `Stock` node type: a descriptor, and a function that prepares a step from the parameters, the steps being closures;
  - the ten nodes, each with its parameters' struct and schema: `BinParameters`, `DemosaicParameters`, `ResizeParameters`, `ColourParameters`, `DisplayParameters` and `JpegParameters`, sharing `LevelsParameter` and `CurveParameter` (`"linear"`, `"srgb"` or `{"gamma": 2.2}`).
- **`port`:** `PortType::Encoded` with its `Encoding`, JPEG; and `Value::Encoded`.
- **`Descriptor::on_cuda`:** every stock node declares both backends except JPEG, which libjpeg-turbo runs on the CPU.
- **A `cuda` feature,** carrying the nodes' GPU paths through `tetera-imaging`.
- **The `recipe` example** runs the live chain on each backend and checks it against the ISP.
- **No new dependencies.**

## Design notes and deviations from the plan

- **Calibrations are inputs:** `tetera.dark` and `tetera.flat` take the master dark and the master flat as inputs beside the frame, so a recipe names where they come from. The recipe subtracts the dark from the master flat with a `tetera.dark` node of its own.
- **The flat field is made once for each master flat,** not for each frame. The step keeps the master its field was made from, and makes a new field when a different master comes. Comparing the two costs a pass over the master; a pipeline that knew the master was unchanged could skip that, and P1.08c's executor work covers it.
- **One level for every channel, or one each:** `tetera.display` takes either. Given one level, it makes its tables for each image's channels. The first version kept 3-channel tables for a grey image that came after an RGB one; the test of grey after RGB found it.
- **The parameters mirror the imaging types,** rather than adding serde to `tetera-imaging`, a layer below. The curve reads as `"linear"`, `"srgb"` or `{"gamma": 2.2}`.
- **Validation finds what the functions would refuse,** where it can, before anything runs: a bin factor outside 1 to 4, a size of zero, a JPEG quality outside 1 to 100, a colour matrix out of range, an impossible gamma.
- **Constant inputs are processed again for every frame:** the master flat's dark subtraction runs each time. Doing once what doesn't change belongs with the executor's work in P1.08c.

## Tests, and the mutants

- **8 new tests,** 7 in every build and 1 on the GPU. `tetera-pipeline` now has 31 without the feature, and 32 with it:
  - the raw nodes against the functions they wrap: binning, by sum, by mean and by default; the Bayer split's four planes; the superpixel; demosaicking, by each method and by default;
  - dark and flat against theirs, the field made once, and again for a new master;
  - resize; colour, with its parameters and its defaults; the display transform, with one level for each channel or one for all, and grey after RGB;
  - JPEG, RGB and grey, the same bytes as `jpeg::encode`;
  - every bad parameter refused while preparing, before anything runs;
  - a node given the wrong value;
  - **the live chain's recipe against `Isp::run`:** the preview identical, and the same JPEG;
  - **on the GPU,** the live chain and every other raw node giving what they give on the CPU.
- **Broken on purpose,** as the spec asks: **the demosaic node ignoring its method.** It failed the test of the nodes against their functions. The GPU's test compares the backends, and a node broken alike on both passes it; the CPU's test is the one that pins each node to its function.
- **Mutants,** with CUDA, over this phase's changes: 59 tested, 21 caught, 38 unviable, none missed. Most of the unviable ones replace a node's preparation with a default its step type doesn't have.
