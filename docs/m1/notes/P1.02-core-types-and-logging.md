## Summary

The vocabulary every crate shares, and logging every example and test can turn on.

- **`tetera-core`** gains:
  - units;
  - geometry typed by unit;
  - the monotonic clock mapped once to UTC, with its drift check;
  - typed IDs that sort by creation;
  - one error type;
  - log records and the ring.
- **`tetera-log`,** a new L1 crate, sets up `tracing` with the ring, JSON files rotated by size, journald (critical at priority 2) and standard error.

## What was built

- **`units`:** `Um` with mm and nm conversions and arithmetic. `duration_from_ms` and `duration_ms` convert at the API's edge, refusing negative, infinite and NaN values.
- **`geometry`:**
  - `Point2<U>` and `Vector2<U>`, typed by `Micrometres` or `Pixels`;
  - `Affine2<From, To>`, with `similarity`, `apply`, `apply_vector`, `then`, `determinant` and `inverse`;
  - `Rect` in whole pixels, its edges in `u64`;
  - `StagePos`;
  - hand-written JSON Schemas for the generic types, named by unit (`PointUm`, `AffinePxToUm`).
- **`time`:**
  - `Timestamp` and `UtcTime`, the latter read and written as RFC 3339 with Hinnant's civil-date algorithms;
  - the `Clock` trait, with `SystemClock` and `ManualClock`;
  - `drift_exceeds_limit` with `DRIFT_LIMIT` at one second.
- **`id`:** `Id<T>` over a UUID v7, as a string in JSON.
- **`error`:**
  - `Error`, with an `ErrorKind`, a message, contexts read outermost first, and a source;
  - `ResultExt::context` and `with_context`.
- **`log`:** `LogLevel` (trace to critical), `LogRecord`, `LogEntry`, and `LogRing` with sequence numbers, `since` for tails, drop counts, and survival of a poisoned lock.
- **`tetera-log`:**
  - `LogConfig`, as `tetera.toml`'s `[log]` will hold it;
  - the decisions as small functions: `under_systemd`, `journald_wanted`, `stderr_wanted`, `filter_directives`;
  - `RecordLayer` and its sinks: the ring; `RotatingFile` and `FileSink`; `JournaldSink` over the native protocol;
  - `build` and `init`, `critical!`, and `warn_on_drift`.
- **Examples:** `core_tour` and `log_tour`, with READMEs.
- **ADRs 0006 and 0007.**

## Ideas taken from OScope

From its documents, in our own words (ADR-0002): a single monotonic clock mapped once to UTC, and µm and ms at every API (OScope's plan, D30 and its review point 11); IDs and errors as foundation types in L0 (OScope ADR-0005's layer table).

## How to try it

```bash
cargo run -p tetera-core --example core_tour
cargo run -p tetera-log --example log_tour
cargo run -p tetera-log --example log_tour -- --journald
journalctl -t tetera-log-tour -o verbose -n 6
```

The crates' READMEs ([tetera-core](../../../crates/tetera-core/README.md), [tetera-log](../../../crates/tetera-log/README.md)) say what each shows.

## Design notes and deviations from the plan

- **The owner's choices (2026-11-26):**
  - the phase was kept whole on an estimate of 3,180;
  - the rotating file and the journald writer were written in-house. `tracing-appender` rotates only by time, and `tracing-journald` maps priority by level only, so it can't send a critical record at CRIT. The spec now says so.
- **`schemars` is always on,** not behind a `schema` feature as the spec first said. Every type crossing the API needs it from P1.13, and a feature no gate turns on would leave the derives unchecked. The spec now says so.
- **No `thiserror`:** `Error` has its own `Display` and `std::error::Error`, about fifteen lines, for one dependency fewer.
- **No UTC crate:** `UtcTime` formats and parses RFC 3339 itself, after Howard Hinnant's *chrono-Compatible Low-Level Date Algorithms*. It covers the years 1678 to 2261.
- **A critical record on standard error** shows as `ERROR` with `critical=true`: the readable layer is `tracing-subscriber`'s own.
- **journald:** a record larger than one datagram (about 200 kB) is dropped, since journald's memfd path isn't implemented. A sink that fails keeps quiet.
- **Ten transitive crates were newer than two weeks** when the lock file was first made: `bumpalo`, `bytes`, `hashbrown`, `pin-project-lite`, `rand`, `regex-automata`, `regex-syntax`, `toml_edit`, `toml_parser`, and `uuid` 1.29.3. Each is pinned back to its newest release from 2026-11-12 or earlier; all 84 crates from crates.io now are. P1.01 had checked only direct dependencies.
- **`.cargo/mutants.toml`** gains one exclusion: `UtcTime::now`'s branch for a wall clock before 1970.
- **`log_tour` without `--smoke` or `--dir`** leaves its log file in a temporary folder, so it can be read afterwards.

## Tests, and the mutants

- **91 tests:** 61 in `tetera-core`, 29 in `tetera-log`, and its doc example.
  - **Property tests:**
    - unit conversions;
    - an affine map then its inverse;
    - composition applying maps in order;
    - rectangle intersections, against membership point by point;
    - `UtcTime` across its whole range.
  - **Exhaustive:** every day from the year −1000 to 3000, through the civil-date algorithms and back, each the day after the one before.
  - **The clock:** monotonic over 10,000 reads; the drift limit at 0.999 999 999 s, 1 s and 1.000 000 001 s, either way.
  - **IDs:** 10,000 in order; 8,000 across 8 threads all different.
  - **The ring:** 8 threads × 1,000 records into 100, with 7,900 dropped and consecutive sequence numbers; a panic while holding it.
  - **Files:** rotation and `keep`, an exact fit at the limit, a line longer than the limit, resuming an existing file.
  - **journald:** the encoding, field names, a newline in a value, and a fake journal socket receiving datagrams, through the sink and through `build`.
  - **The decisions:** journald and standard error on `Auto`, `RUST_LOG`, a bad filter, a missing journal on `Auto` against `On`, a log folder that can't be made.
- **Broken on purpose:** the ring's dropping of its oldest record, and the drift limit's comparison. Their mutants (`pop_front` removed, `>` against `>=`, `+=` against `-=` on the drop count) are caught.
- **Mutants:**
  - the first run on both crates tested 733: **34 missed and 4 timeouts.** The misses were tests too weak to tell the code from its mutant: a dot product only tested against `(1, 0)`, a similarity's coefficients only at a quarter turn, a separator check with no right-length input, a rotation with no exact fit, the civil-date algorithms only sampled. The timeouts were tests waiting on a fake journal that never received anything;
  - tests were added for each, and those waits given a five-second limit;
  - now: **732 tested, 671 caught, 61 unviable, none missed, no timeouts.**
