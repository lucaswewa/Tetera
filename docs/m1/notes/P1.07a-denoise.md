## Behaviour spec

Written before the code, as a spec-first phase's must be.

### What noise there is

A camera frame carries **shot noise**, the Poisson statistics of the photons, whose standard deviation grows as the square root of the signal. It also carries **read noise**, roughly constant and Gaussian. Both are independent from frame to frame, and nearly so from pixel to pixel. Denoising trades them against detail: averaging more samples lowers the noise, but samples from elsewhere carry other detail.

### Temporal averaging

While the stage is still, successive frames see the same scene, so averaging them reduces independent noise by √N for N frames without blurring anything. That is the standard result for the mean of independent samples.

- **What it computes:** the mean of the last N frames since the stage last moved, at each site of the raw mosaic. Averaging is linear, so it belongs before the ISP, where it costs least and treats every site alike.
- **The window:** the exact mean of the most recent `min(n, N)` frames, `n` being the frames since the reset.
  - It is not an exponential average: the result is exactly the mean of a known set of frames, which a later step (or a user) can reason about.
  - It keeps the last N frames, a ring, and their sum.
- **The reset:** a move of the stage, a change of mode or layout, or a request clears the window. The next frame starts it again, so the first frame after a move is shown alone, at full noise, rather than blended with the old scene. The caller says when the stage moved; the preview pipeline (P1.14a) knows from the stage.
- **Rounding:** the sum is kept exactly in 32 bits, and the mean rounded half up, `(sum + n/2) / n`. So the CPU twin and the CUDA kernel agree exactly.

### An edge-preserving spatial filter: bilateral, not non-local means

The spec offers two (Q20):
- **the bilateral filter** (C. Tomasi and R. Manduchi, "Bilateral filtering for gray and color images", ICCV 1998) weighs each neighbour by its distance and by how alike its value is, so it averages within a flat region but not across an edge;
- **non-local means** (A. Buades, B. Coll and J.-M. Morel, "A non-local algorithm for image denoising", CVPR 2005) weighs pixels across a search window by how alike their surrounding patches are. It preserves texture better.

**Weighed at 20 MP:**
- **non-local means' usual parameters,** a 21 × 21 search of 7 × 7 patches, cost about 21,600 operations a pixel, some 430 billion a frame: seconds on this GPU, minutes on the CPU;
- **a bilateral filter of radius 3,** 7 × 7 taps, costs 49 a pixel, 1 billion a frame: milliseconds on the GPU.

Tetera's denoising runs on live frames and in batch alike, so **the bilateral filter is the stock spatial filter**. Non-local means, BM3D and learned denoisers are for extensions (M2).

**The bilateral filter, as Tetera computes it,** on an RGB image of 16-bit values:

- **Weights:** a neighbour at offset (dx, dy), within the radius r in each direction, weighs `w_s(dx, dy) × w_r(d)`, where
  - `w_s = exp(−(dx² + dy²) / (2 σ_s²))`, the spatial weight;
  - `w_r = exp(−d² / (2 σ_r²))`, the range weight;
  - `d` is the colour difference, `(|ΔR| + 2 |ΔG| + |ΔB|) / 4`: one weight for all three channels, so the filter doesn't shift hue at an edge (the joint, colour form of Tomasi and Manduchi's §3).
- **Its output:** each channel's weighted mean.
- **The radius:** `r = ⌈2 σ_s⌉`, at most 7.
- **The edges:** the image is mirrored without repeating the edge, as demosaicking does.
- **In fixed point:** both weights are tables made once on the host, the spatial one in 1/32768ths per offset, the range one in 1/32768ths per difference from 0 to 65,535. A neighbour's weight is their product. Each channel's sum of weight × value, and the sum of weights, are exact in 64 bits, and the mean is rounded half up. So the twins agree exactly, though the tables come from `exp`.
- **Parameters:** `σ_s` in pixels, and `σ_r` in the image's units. For noise of standard deviation σ, `σ_r` of about 2σ smooths the noise and keeps edges higher than that.

### What the tests check

- **The temporal mean:** exact over 1 to N frames; the oldest frame leaving the window; the reset on a move, and on a new layout.
- **The bilateral filter:**
  - its tables: the spatial table peaks at the centre and is symmetric, and the range table falls with the difference;
  - a flat image is unchanged;
  - an edge of height well above σ_r stays an edge;
  - noise falls in a flat region;
  - a constant colour stays that colour.
- **PSNR:** a noisy synthetic image's PSNR rises under each method. The numbers are recorded under Performance.
- **Twins:** both, exactly.
- **Broken on purpose:**
  - without the reset, a moved scene's frames blend with the old;
  - without the range weight, an edge blurs.

## Summary

Tetera can now reduce noise, on the CPU or on CUDA, and the two agree exactly.
- **Temporal averaging:** the mean of the last N frames, reset when the stage moves. On a frame with noise of σ 80 in 12 bits, averaging 8 frames raises the PSNR by **9.0 dB**, the √8 the behaviour spec predicts. It takes 1.0 ms a frame on the GPU at 20 MP.
- **The bilateral filter,** the stock edge-preserving filter, as the spec chose over non-local means: smooth regions lose their noise, and edges higher than its range σ stay sharp.

The behaviour spec above was committed before the code, in `4ce26bd`.

## Performance

A full frame, 5,472 × 3,648, 12 bits, noise σ 80, from `denoise` in release, the best of 3:

| | PSNR before | PSNR after | CPU, 16 threads | CUDA |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Temporal mean of 8 raw frames | 34.19 dB | 43.22 dB | 27 ms a frame | 1.05 ms a frame |
| Bilateral, σ_s 1, σ_r 160, on one demosaicked frame | 34.57 dB | 35.83 dB | 345 ms | 17.8 ms |

**On the test pattern at 96 × 96,** the bilateral filter raised the PSNR by 2 to 4 dB over the whole image, and by 4 to 10 dB over its smooth top band, for noise of σ 40 to 120. The zone plate's rings are finer than its window, so it averages some of them away with the noise. It gains less on a demosaicked full frame (1.3 dB), whose noise demosaicking has already spread across neighbours.

**σ_s 1 and σ_r 2σ** did best of the parameters measured, and are the example's.

## Design notes and deviations from the plan

- **The bilateral filter, not non-local means:** the behaviour spec's choice, on cost at 20 MP.
- **Averaging the raw mosaic:** temporal averaging works on the raw frame, before the ISP, where it is linear and cheapest. The spatial filter works on RGB, after demosaicking.
- **Exact, not approximate:** the window's sum is exact in 32 bits, which caps it at 64 frames. The filter's weights are fixed-point tables made once on the host from `exp`, so the twins agree exactly though the weights are Gaussians.
- **The CPU twin walks the window by its coordinates,** not by offsets from the centre. Walked by offsets, a mutant reading `x − dx` for `x + dx` sums the same, since the spatial weights are point-symmetric, and no test could tell it apart.
