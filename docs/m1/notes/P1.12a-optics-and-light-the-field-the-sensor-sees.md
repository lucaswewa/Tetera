## Summary

Tetera knows the light that reaches the virtual camera's sensor: the simulated slide through the fitted objective, its defocus, vignetting and the illumination, on the GPU and on the CPU. The phase's *Done when* holds:
- **The field of view matches the magnification and the mounting angle:** a pixel is 2.4 µm over the magnification; turned by the mounting angle; its box the box of its four corners at any angle.
- **The blur grows with the distance to focus,** as the spec's model says: the Airy disc's in focus, the circle of confusion's added in quadrature. A field in focus is the sharpest, more than twice as sharp as 10 µm away.
- **The twins agree:** the GPU's field within 4e-5 of the light's intensity of the CPU's, at 5×, 20× and 50×, turned and in focus or not.
- **A full 5472 × 3648 field's time on the GPU, against the 40 ms budget** (§6.15):

  | Objective | Still | Moving 100 pixels a frame |
  |---|---|---|
  | 5× | 9.3 ms | 9 to 36 ms |
  | 20× | 18.9 ms | 24 to 25 ms |
  | 50× | 38.6 ms | 41 to 46 ms |

  Still, every objective is within the budget. Moving, 50× runs 1 to 6 ms over: its three depth groups' newly visible tiles. P1.12b measures jogging, and reports it against the budget.
- **[ADR-0016](../../adr/0016-the-virtual-cameras-model.md)** records the virtual camera's model.

## Performance

- **Still frames reuse everything:** the tiles are cached and the planes kept, so a still frame resamples, blurs and lights, nothing more. 9 ms at 5×, 19 ms at 20×, 39 ms at 50×, whose three depth groups each blur by up to 11 pixels.
- **Moving frames make the tiles newly in view.** At 5×, a tile is 82 µm, so moving 100 pixels (48 µm) a frame makes a column of tiles every other frame: 36 ms, then 9. At 50×, each of three groups makes its own column.
- **Cold, a new place makes all its tiles:** 0.3 to 0.9 s.
- **What it took:**
  - a field was 85 ms at 50× before; most of it was allocating and zeroing fresh 80 MB planes in each step. Kept from frame to frame, the planes brought 50× to 41 ms, and 5× from 30 to 9 ms;
  - blurs in shared memory then brought 50× to 38 ms.
- **The CPU fallback,** 912 × 608 pixels, takes 0.2 to 0.4 s a field; tests use 192 × 128.

## Design notes and deviations from the plan

- **The field is light, not counts:** photoelectrons a ms at quantum efficiency 1, in red, green and blue. The sensor (P1.12b) samples it in RGGB, with its own quantum efficiency and noise.
- **Depth groups by the depth of field,** to whole 0.5 µm layers: one group at 5× and 10×, two at 20×, three at 50×. Each group's tiles are one tile key, so a still frame reuses them all.
- **Stains are blurred, then lit:** blur acts on optical densities, which add over depth, and Beer–Lambert follows. It is the thin-specimen approximation ADR-0016 records.
- **The CPU renders the region the tiles cover and resamples it the same way,** so the twins agree within 4e-5 of the light; half precision's rounding dominates.
- **A tile table on the GPU:** the resampling kernel reads each tile through its address, so the tiles stay where the cache put them; nothing is copied into one mosaic.
- **Blurs in shared memory** when the halo fits in 40 KB, global memory beyond; a test runs both paths against the host's blur.
- **The light is uncorrected:** at 3,200 K a raw frame is warm. White balance is the ISP's (P1.06).
- **The first group's blur writes the sum; the others add,** so the sum needs no zeroing each frame.

## Tests, and the mutants

- **25 new tests:** 20 in `tetera-sim` (77 there now) and 5 in `tetera-compute` (89).
  - **optics:**
    - an objective's numbers checked;
    - the field of view against the magnification and the angle, its box at any angle;
    - depth groups for each objective, blurrier away from focus;
    - the blur growing with defocus, symmetric, and the Airy disc's in focus;
    - a Gaussian's weights;
    - blackbody colours, warm and cool, and at 20,000 K where Planck's −1 matters;
    - unevenness and vignetting;
    - a grid's tiles counted exactly, and bilinear held at its edges;
    - a pixel lit by its place and its stains;
    - the grid covering its field;
    - the field sharpest in focus, bare glass as bright as the light, the same twice;
    - changing the objective changing the field;
  - **the light:** its state becoming the field's light, off and on, checked;
  - **on the GPU, in `tetera-sim`:**
    - the twins at 5×, 20× and 50×, two sizes of sensor through one cache;
    - a full field's time, cold, still and moved;
  - **on the GPU, in `tetera-compute`:**
    - resampling a synthetic tile bilinearly, turned, held at its edge;
    - tiles and planes that don't fit refused;
    - the blur in shared memory and global memory, the same as the host's, adding up;
    - the light by Beer–Lambert.
- **Broken on purpose,** as the spec asks: **the defocus model inverted,** sharpest 6 µm from focus. Three tests caught it:
  - the blur growing with defocus;
  - the depth groups' order;
  - the field sharpest in focus: 104,824 in focus against 565,532 out of it.
- **Run 15 times,** the tests passed each time.
- **Mutants,** of both crates with CUDA on and the GPU tests enabled:
  - **the first run missed 68.** Most were in `tetera-compute`'s launches, which cargo-mutants tested only with that crate's own tests, while the field's were all in `tetera-sim`.
    - The kernels now have tests of their own, and later runs also ran `tetera-sim`'s field tests for compute's mutants (`--test-package`).
    - The planes are checked before each launch, so a plane allocated too small is refused rather than overrun, and a test hands them planes of the wrong size.
    - Tests for the rest: the field's box at any angle, Planck's −1, `srgb8`'s values, the tiles' count, bilinear's edges, a pixel's light, a cache through two sensor sizes.
  - **The last runs missed none:** 40 caught in the launches and 393 in `tetera-sim`. 111 timed out, each a mutant that made a field's grid or blur enormous, so the tests ran long.
  - **Three exclusions, with their reasons in `.cargo/mutants.toml`:**
    - the CPU blur read the other way round, its weights being symmetric;
    - its sign flipped in the one closure that makes both passes, which flips back;
    - `gaussian`'s boundary at exactly 1e-3.

    The shared-memory limit is now one literal, `40_960`, rather than an excluded product.
