## Summary

`tetera-jobs`, a new L3 crate, runs long work that can be followed, cancelled and looked up afterwards ([ADR-0020](../../adr/0020-the-job-service.md)): hardware jobs one at a time from a queue, compute jobs on a pool that shares the GPU's memory through a VRAM budget, each with its progress, log and result, and a history in SQLite. The server has one, and the `jobs` Thing over it. The phase's *Done when* holds:

- **Hardware jobs never overlap:** six queued at once ran one at a time, in the order they came; in the example, each began as the one before ended.
- **A cancelled job stops within a second:** one queued ends at once and never runs; one running ends when its work next checks its token, 13 to 14 ms after the ask in the example, and well within the second in the tests, also while it waits for VRAM.
- **The history survives a restart, and old entries are pruned:** a job's end, its result or error, and its log are there after the service opens again; a job left running by a crash is marked failed, interrupted; ended jobs older than the history's age go when it opens and as jobs end.

## What was built

- **`crates/tetera-jobs`,** L3, on `tetera-core` and `tetera-compute`:
  - **`Job`:** an id (a UUID v7, so ids sort by age), its kind (hardware or compute), its name, its state (queued, running, succeeded, failed, cancelled), progress and a message, its latest log lines, its result or error, whether its cancel was asked, the VRAM it asked for, and when it was made, started and ended;
  - **`JobService`:**
    - `submit` queues a closure given a `JobContext`, as a hardware or a compute job;
    - one worker for hardware jobs; a pool of compute workers, two by default, each job taking its VRAM from the compute jobs' budget before it runs, and waiting while others hold it;
    - `get`, `active`, `recent`, `wait`, `subscribe` (every change to every job), `cancel`;
    - stopping cancels what is queued, asks what runs to stop, and waits for it;
    - a job that panics fails with its panic's message;
  - **`JobContext`:** `progress`, `log`, and `check` and `sleep`, which return the cancel error once the job is asked to stop;
  - **`History`:** the jobs in SQLite, each as JSON beside its state and times; the interrupted marked failed when it opens; the ended pruned by age.
- **In `tetera-api`:**
  - **the `jobs` Thing:** `active`, kept current; the `changed` event, every change to a job; `get`, `list` and `cancel`;
  - **`build_with`** and `JobsOptions`, the server's `[jobs]`: compute workers, days kept, and the compute jobs' VRAM;
  - **the instrument keeps the GPU's budgets:** three quarters of its memory, from which the camera reserves its share and the compute jobs theirs, 1 GiB by default.
- **`tetera-server`** reads `[jobs]`; `configs/tetera.toml` documents it. The history is the data root's `tetera.db`.
- **The example `jobs`,** the crate's README, and ADR-0020.
- **A new dependency:** `rusqlite` 0.42.0, with SQLite built in (`bundled`), released 2026-11-11, MIT. Its own new dependencies are older still; SQLite itself is in the public domain.

## How to try it

```bash
cargo run --release -p tetera-jobs --example jobs
```

It queues four hardware jobs and four compute jobs, cancels one of each, stops the service, opens it again, reads the history back, and then prunes it. `-- --smoke` runs it briefly.

## Performance

- **A cancel takes effect when the work next checks:** `JobContext::sleep` checks every 10 ms, so the example's running job stopped 13 to 14 ms after the ask.
- **Each job is written to the history four times at most:** when it is queued, when it starts, when its cancel is asked, and when it ends. Progress and log lines stay in memory until it ends.
- **Pruning is one indexed `DELETE`** each time a job ends.

## Design notes and deviations from the plan

- **Cancel is cooperative,** as Rust can't stop a thread from outside: work that ends with an error after it was asked to stop is cancelled; work that ends well despite the ask has succeeded.
- **The history's writes and the jobs in memory share one lock order,** jobs then history. The example first hung on a race: a cancel wrote the job as it stood, running, just after the job's own end had written it cancelled, and the history kept the stale state. A test now cancels forty short jobs as they end.
- **The job leaves memory only after its end is saved, pruned and told,** so whoever sees it ended finds it in the history, and has heard its end.
- **The workers wait for notifications alone,** with no timeout to fall back on: `wake` takes each queue's lock before it notifies, so none is missed.
- **The instrument's VRAM budgets** are now three quarters of the GPU's memory, shared between the camera and the compute jobs. Before, the camera had a budget of its own size alone.
- **The jobs Thing's reads are actions,** `get` and `list`, as teta-wot's properties take no arguments.
- **No work runs as a job yet:** captures (P1.15b), autofocus (P1.16), scans (P1.17) and batches (P1.19) will.

## Tests, and the mutants

- **13 new tests:**
  - **the service:** a job from queued to running to its end, with its progress, at most 1, its log and result, and its events in order; a failing job and two panicking ones failing with their reasons; hardware jobs one at a time in order; the compute pool's limit and its VRAM, a job asking too much refused, one asking exactly the budget run; cancel while queued, while running, and after the end, and work that misses its token; a compute job cancelled while it waits for VRAM; the history across a stop, a crash's job marked interrupted, pruning when it opens and as jobs end, an unfinished job never pruned; a stopped service taking no more jobs; a job's log kept to its latest lines; bad configurations refused; a cancel racing a job's end;
  - **the `jobs` Thing over HTTP:** its events and `active` as a job runs, `get`, `list`, `cancel`, and unknown or ended jobs refused.
- **Broken on purpose,** as the spec asks:
  - **cancel,** the token never checked: three tests failed, jobs still running 10 s after their cancel, and a stop that took more than a second;
  - **the one-at-a-time rule,** two hardware workers: three tests failed, two jobs at once, and out of order;
  - and **the history's write race,** read and written under separate locks: the race test failed, a job still running 2 s after it ended.
- **Run 15 times,** the three crates' tests, `tetera-jobs`', `tetera-api`'s and `tetera-server`'s, 58 in all, passed each time.
- **Mutants:** 
  - **`tetera-jobs`' first run,** 96 tested: 52 caught, 24 unviable, 2 timeouts, 18 missed. Most were tests too weak: the context's id, a success's progress made whole, the history's writes before a job ends, a running job's cancel leaving it running, dropping the service. Three changed the code instead:
    - **the workers waited with a timeout,** 100 ms, and VRAM waits 50 ms, so a missed notification only cost time and `wake` could be emptied unseen. They now wait for notifications alone, and `wake` takes each queue's lock before it notifies, so a worker between its look and its wait can't miss one. `cancel` wakes too, for a job waiting for VRAM;
    - **`cancel` compared a queue's length** before and after removing the job; it now finds the job's position;
    - **the start's log line** was written only when something was interrupted or pruned; it is always written.
  - **`tetera-api`'s first run,** over the change, missed `JobsOptions`' arithmetic, the jobs Thing's stop hook (as with the statistics, the test now checks its thread), and a shift on the GPU's path that only a CUDA run reaches.
  - **`JobService::stop` emptied** joins the preview's and the statistics' in `.cargo/mutants.toml`: dropping it halts it the same.
  - **The last runs:** `tetera-jobs` 80 tested, 53 caught, 24 unviable, 3 timeouts; `tetera-api` over the change, with the GPU's line under CUDA; **none missed**.
