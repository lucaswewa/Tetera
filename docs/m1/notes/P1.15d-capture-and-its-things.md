## Summary

Captures land on disk as datasets, in projects, without losing a frame. `tetera-workflows`, a new L4 crate, takes one frame, N frames at an interval, or a burst at the camera's full rate into a dataset, switching the camera from its binned live mode to full resolution and back (D5). The `capture` Thing runs it as a hardware job, and the `datasets` Thing lists, reads back and deletes what it made. The phase's *Done when* holds:

- **A capture switches the mode and back:** on the GPU, a burst of 20 full frames went to full resolution in 577 to 672 ms and back to the live view in 34 to 126 ms, which then streamed on in its binned mode. Each kind of capture puts the camera back as it was, in the tests, whether it succeeds, fails or is cancelled.
- **A capture cancelled through the `jobs` Thing leaves no half-written dataset in the index:** a burst and a series, each cancelled over HTTP once they had frames, left no row in the index, written or being written, no dataset's folder, and no frame log.

## What was built

- **`tetera-workflows`** (new, L4): **`capture(rig, request, step)`**.
  - **The request:** the project, the dataset's name, and what to take: `single`, `series` (`count`, `interval_ms`) or `burst` (`count`). The camera's mode is 0 unless named, at 12 bits and the live view's exposure. A request that can't be taken, or a project that isn't there, is refused before the camera is touched.
  - **The switch:** the camera stopped if it streams, set to the capture's mode, and snapped once, which builds the mode's buffers; afterwards set back as it was and restarted with the live view's sink. Both times are measured.
  - **A frame or a series** snapped into a dataset writer; **a burst** recorded through the frame bus into a frame log on the scratch disk, then its first `count` frames ingested, the log removed either way.
  - **The step,** called as it goes: an error from it cancels the capture, and nothing is left.
- **In `tetera-api`:**
  - **the `capture` Thing:** `capture` queues a capture as a hardware job and answers with the job; `last` is the last capture's result. The live view restarts through the `camera` Thing's sink, so its frame count goes on;
  - **the `datasets` Thing:** `projects`; `create_project`, `list`, `get` (the dataset, its files, its metadata and its thumbnail's path) and `delete`;
  - **the thumbnail's route,** `/api/v1/datasets/{id}/thumbnail.jpg`: a 404 for a dataset that isn't there, a 409 for one being written;
  - **the server** opens the store in the data root as a service, its index beside the jobs' history in `tetera.db`, and the two Things make ten;
  - **the instrument** describes itself for a dataset's metadata, and gives a sensor pixel's size at the sample through the objective fitted;
  - **the example `capture`**.
- **In `tetera-store`:** `Store::project`, and `ingest_first`, which ingests the first N frames of a log: a burst's recorder can take a frame or two more than asked before the camera stops.
- **In `tetera-acquire`:** the recorder counts a dropped frame once. The camera both skips its number and counts it in the next frame's `dropped_before`; the recorder had added the two.
- **No new dependencies.**

## Performance

- **The switch to full resolution,** on the GPU, took 577 to 672 ms in three quiet runs: the mode's first frame builds its buffers, as P1.13b's notes found. The switch back, with the live view restarted, took 34 to 126 ms.
- **A burst of 20 full frames,** 5472 × 3648 at 12 bits, at the camera's 7.2 frames a second, none lost: 798 MB of raw frames recorded, then ingested into 522 MB of OME-Zarr with zstd, 1.5 : 1, the whole job about 11 s.
- **Straight after a build,** the system disk is slow enough to hold the recorder, and the camera drops frames: 17 of 37 in one run. The recorder loses none, and `missing` says how many the camera did (Q14: a scratch disk of its own).
- **On the CPU,** the virtual camera can't render a 912 × 608 frame at mode 0's rate: a burst of 5 lost 26 frames, each counted once in `missing`. The smoke run uses the tiny sensor, which keeps up.

## Design notes and deviations from the plan

- **Capture lives in `tetera-workflows`,** L4, over the camera's trait, the frame bus and the store, with no knowledge of jobs or Things: its step closure carries a job's progress and cancel check, as ingest's does (P1.15c).
- **A burst is recorded, then ingested,** rather than written straight into the dataset: the frame log keeps up with the camera's full rate on this disk (P1.15c), and ingest, at about 120 MB/s, doesn't. Both run within the one hardware job, so the instrument is free when it ends.
- **A burst counts frames on the bus,** not in the recorder, and stops the camera once enough are published. A frame or two more may reach the log; `ingest_first` takes the first `count`.
- **The project is checked first,** before the camera is switched: otherwise a burst into a missing project would record every frame only to be refused at ingest.
- **The dataset's pixel size** is a sensor pixel's at the sample, times the mode's binning: a capture in the binned mode records 0.48 µm at 20×, not 0.24.
- **`missing` counts each drop once:** the camera's dropped frame skips its number and adds to the next frame's `dropped_before`, so the recorder now takes the larger of the two for each frame, not their sum.
- **The live view's sink:** a capture restarts the stream with the `camera` Thing's own sink, so `camera/frames` and `camera/last_frame` go on. Without a Thing, it restarts into the bus.
- **Known limit:** the camera's own properties and actions aren't locked during a capture. Changing the camera's mode over HTTP while a capture runs would spoil it. The job service runs one hardware job at a time; the `camera` Thing's setters don't go through it (see Follow-ups).
- **Deleting a dataset being written** is refused as busy: the store's `dataset` already refuses one, and `delete` goes through it.

## Tests, and the mutants

- **10 new tests:**
  - **capture, on a small virtual camera on the CPU:** a single frame at full resolution, its format, pixel size and the instrument in its metadata, and the live view back; a series' interval; a burst's count, each gap in its frames' numbers the camera's own drop; a capture in the binned mode at 8 bits and its own exposure; a burst and a series cancelled at three stages, leaving no dataset, folder or frame log, and the camera streaming as it was; bad requests, an unknown mode and an unknown project refused, the camera put back, and nothing recorded for a project that isn't there;
  - **over HTTP:** a project made and listed; a burst taken as a job while the live view runs, its result in `last`, the live view back and counting; the dataset listed, read back with its files, metadata and thumbnail, served as a JPEG, and deleted; refusals made no job; a burst and a series cancelled through the `jobs` Thing, leaving nothing in the index, being written or not, nor on disk;
  - **on the fake camera,** which offers what the virtual one doesn't: the capture mode clearing the live view's region and binning, at 12 bits, and a refused switch put back; a series' frames never early, its waits said, and each step's progress; a burst's two dropped frames counted once, and its progress as it records and as it ingests;
  - **over HTTP, besides:** the dataset's pixel size through the objective fitted; a dataset being written has no thumbnail yet, a 409, and none once its writer drops, a 404;
  - **the recorder:** a drop told both by its number and by the camera, counted once; **the store:** a project by name.
- **Broken on purpose,** as the spec asks: **the clean-up after a cancelled capture.**
  - The burst's frame log left behind: three tests failed, "no frame log left" and "the frame log removed".
  - The camera not put back after a failed or cancelled capture: two failed, the camera left in mode 0 at 12 bits.
- **Run 15 times:** the four crates' tests, `tetera-workflows`', `tetera-api`'s, `tetera-store`'s and `tetera-acquire`'s, 78 in all, passed 15 of 15. The first round passed 14: the fake camera's series checked each gap at 140 ms or more, but a frame is due an interval after the one before counted from the first, so after one late frame on a busy CPU the next catches up. Each frame is now checked against when it was due.
- **Mutants:** 
  - **the first run,** over the change, 102 tested: 50 caught, 23 unviable, 3 timeouts, **26 missed.** The capture's progress arithmetic; a series' interval, shorter than a snap of mode 0 on the CPU, so nothing showed it; the switches' times, checked only as above 0; the 12 bits, as 8 bits are two bytes a sample too; the region and binning cleared, which the virtual camera doesn't offer; the exposure check's edges, as a later step refused the same values; and the thumbnail of a dataset being written.
  - **Tests now cover them,** most on the fake camera, with one exclusion: `<` and `<=` in a series' wait differ only at the exact nanosecond.
  - **The second run** missed the dataset's pixel size through the objective, over HTTP, now checked. Its `tetera-workflows` mutants were all unviable: the next phase's example, drafted while it ran, lay in the tree without its modules, and `cargo test` builds examples. The drafts were moved out, and the capture run again.
  - **The third run,** over the capture and the instrument, 71 tested, missed the switch's time computed with `+ 1e3`, which the virtual camera's slow snap hid when the CPU was busy. The fake camera's quick switch now bounds it.
  - **The last run,** over the capture, 65 tested: 54 caught, 10 unviable, **none missed,** and 1 timeout: the series' wait turned to `now > next` spins until each test's deadline of 20 s, longer in all than the 60 s allowed.
