## Summary

Tetera has a clock that tests control, and moves that go the way a real stage's do.
- **`SimClock`** runs at real time, N× as fast, or only when stepped. Simulated devices wait on it, and a step wakes them.
- **`Profile`** is a straight move from rest to rest, trapezoidal or triangular, with each axis kept to its own limits. Its position comes from closed forms, so the same times give the same positions, bit for bit.
- **A feed hold** slows a move to rest from wherever it has got to.
- **The *Done when* holds:**
  - property tests hold for 400 random moves and 400 random holds on each run;
  - the clock never goes back, at any rate;
  - a step wakes what waits.
- **[ADR-0014](../../adr/0014-the-simulated-clock.md)** records the simulated clock.

## What was built

## Design notes and deviations from the plan

- **A simulated clock's UTC is its own:** its wall clock is its own mapping, so it never drifts. ADR-0006 asked whether a simulated clock needs more of `Clock`: it doesn't. Waiting is on `SimClock` itself.
- **Every wait has a limit in the host's time,** so a device waiting on a stepped clock nobody steps can still notice it is being dropped.
- **A profile is a list of segments:**
  - speeding up from rest;
  - cruising;
  - slowing down to rest, written from its end, so it never overshoots.

  A hold cuts the list where the move has got to, and adds a slowing down from there.
- **Durations round up to the nanosecond:** truncated, a move's duration fell a fraction of a nanosecond short of its end, and it was not quite at rest by then.
- **The path's speed and acceleration** are the most each axis allows for its share of the line, and the speed asked for. A move whose ramps cover its length is a triangle.
- **Moves start and end at rest.** grblHAL blends queued moves; Tetera's stage doesn't queue moves (ADR-0013).

## Tests, and the mutants

- **15 tests,** in every build:
  - **the clock:**
    - stepped, it moves only when stepped, with its own UTC;
    - running, it follows the host at its rate and refuses steps;
    - a rate is above zero;
    - it never goes back, and a stepped clock stops at its end;
    - a step wakes what waits;
    - a running clock waits as long as its rate says, and no longer than its limit;
  - **the profiles:**
    - a long move's trapezoid, at seven times;
    - a short move's triangle;
    - the most limited axis setting the pace;
    - a move to where it is;
    - bad limits, speeds and targets refused;
    - holds while speeding up, cruising, slowing down, done, and before the start;
    - the same times giving the same positions, bit for bit;
  - **property tests,** 400 cases each:
    - **any move:** it starts and ends at rest, where it should, and partway it is on its line. Its duration is the trapezoid's, worked out on its own in the test. Sampled 200 times, it never goes back, and no axis goes faster or changes speed faster than it may;
    - **any hold:** the same until the hold; at rest within its slowing distance, on its line, no later than the move would have ended; and the same limits.
- **The property tests' regressions** are kept in `crates/tetera-sim/proptest-regressions`, so the cases that once failed run first.
- **Broken on purpose,** as the spec asks: **a profile that starts slowing down late,** its cruise 5% too long. The property test caught it at once: a duration of 52.76 s against the trapezoid's 50.26 s.
- **Two problems the property tests found while writing:**
  - the duration truncated to the nanosecond, so a move wasn't at rest by its end;
  - samples at whole nanoseconds, which the speed bounds now allow for.
- **Run 15 times,** the tests passed each time.
- **Mutants:** 138 in the phase's diff; **130 caught, 8 unviable, none missed.**
  - **The first run missed 15:**
    - **untested:** the clock's origin; a profile's start; positions partway along every axis; and where a held move rests. Each has a check now.
    - **code that could be simpler:** a held profile's speed, chosen two ways though both gave its speed at the hold; and a guard that kept a cruise out of a held profile, though one ending at the hold is never reached. Both are gone; `position` and `hold` now share one point along the line.
  - **One exclusion, with its reason in `.cargo/mutants.toml`:** `<` against `<=` where a profile's segments meet, which give the same distance and speed there.
