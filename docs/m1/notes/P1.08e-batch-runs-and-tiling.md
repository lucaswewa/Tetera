## Summary

The pipeline engine is whole: recipes now run over lists of inputs, and over images in tiles.
- **Tiled and untiled results are the same,** exactly, as the phase's *Done when* asks:
  - a raw chain (dark, demosaic, the bilateral filter, colour), tiled five ways, among them tiles that don't divide the frame, tiles larger than it, and tiles of 2 × 2;
  - images through filters one after another;
  - on the GPU against the CPU's whole frame;
  - four 20 MP frames in the example, in tiles of 1,024 × 1,024.
- **Each step says how far it reaches,** from its parameters, or that it needs the whole image. A recipe reaches the longest path's sum, and its tiles take a halo that wide.
- **Batches** run a plan over a list of inputs in order, so a stateful node carries its state from one to the next.

This ends P1.08. Its five phases came to 7,132 lines measured, against 5,070 at its second estimate before the split, and 1,880 at its first.

## What was built

- **`Step::reach`:** how far an output pixel reaches into its inputs, or `None` for a step that needs the whole image or changes its size, the default. The stock nodes declare it:
  - dark, colour, the display transform and temporal averaging, 0;
  - demosaicking, 1 bilinear and 2 Malvar-He-Cutler;
  - the bilateral filter, its radius, ⌈2σ⌉;
  - the rest need the whole image: flat-fielding (its field is made from its master's mean), binning, the superpixel, the Bayer split, resizing, Richardson–Lucy, crop, statistics, JPEG.
- **`tile`** in `tetera-pipeline`:
  - `Plan::run_batch`, over a list of inputs, an error naming its item;
  - `Plan::reach`, the longest path's sum, or the node that stops tiling;
  - `Plan::run_tiled`: the frames and images cut into tiles with their halo, each tile run, each tile's own part stitched back.
- **`recipes/denoise.json`:** the bilateral filter, then the display transform.
- **The `recipe` example** writes a folder of frames as 16-bit PPM, reads it back, and runs the denoise recipe over it as a batch, whole and tiled.
- **No new dependencies.**

## Design notes and deviations from the plan

- **Reach is the step's, not the descriptor's,** since it can depend on parameters: a bilateral filter's radius comes from its σ, and demosaicking's from its method.
- **The halo is even,** rounded up from the recipe's reach, and so is the tile size: a raw frame cut at even columns and rows keeps its Bayer pattern.
- **A tile at the frame's edge** is the frame's edge too, so a node mirrors there as it does for the whole frame. Within the frame, the halo holds the true neighbours of the tile's own part, and what a node does at the halo's outer edge falls outside the part kept.
- **What tiles can't run is refused before any tile runs:**
  - a node that needs the whole image;
  - a stateful node, whose state would pass from tile to tile;
  - held inputs, which a tiled run doesn't cut;
  - frames and images of different sizes;
  - an output that isn't a frame or an image of the tile's size.
- **Flat-fielding needs the whole image:** a tile's field would be evened out by the tile's own mean, not the master's.
- **A batch runs in order,** one input at a time; running a batch's items in parallel, and over a dataset as a job, is P1.19's.

## Tests, and the mutants

- **7 new tests,** 6 in every build and 1 on the GPU. `tetera-pipeline` now has 51 without the feature, and 58 with it:
  - **a raw chain in tiles** against the whole frame: both demosaicking methods, five tile sizes, and 2 × 2 tiles on a small frame; its reach, 4 or 5;
  - **images in tiles,** three filters on two paths, reaching 3 + 4 along the longest;
  - **what tiles can't run:** flat-fielding, temporal averaging, held inputs, sizes that differ;
  - **an output tiles can't stitch,** and inputs without a frame or image;
  - **a batch** of four frames through temporal averaging, in order, and an error naming its item;
  - **on the GPU,** tiles giving the CPU's whole frame.
- **Broken on purpose,** as the spec asks: **a halo one pixel short.** It failed both tiling tests.
