## Summary

A raw Bayer frame now becomes full-colour RGB, on the CPU or on CUDA, and the two agree bit for bit. There are three ways: the half-resolution superpixel, bilinear, and Malvar–He–Cutler.

On a full frame of the test pattern, Malvar–He–Cutler scores **36.1 dB** against bilinear's **31.0 dB**, a gain of 5.1 dB, about what its paper reports. On the GPU, a full frame takes **0.74 ms** and 0.55 ms respectively, against 93 ms and 44 ms on 16 CPU threads.

## What was built

- **`tetera-imaging`,** a new L1 crate, without unsafe code:
  - **`image`:**
    - `Mosaic`, with its `MosaicLayout` (size, pattern, significant bits). It comes from a raw frame, Bayer16 or Bayer8; mirrors at its edges; and crops, moving its pattern for an odd column or row;
    - `Image<T>`, interleaved channels;
    - `BayerPlanes`;
    - `DeviceMosaic` and `DeviceImage` with the `cuda` feature.
  - **`bayer`:** `mosaic` (RGB sampled into a mosaic), and `split` and `superpixel`, each with its `_cpu` twin; `superpixel_device`.
  - **`demosaic`:** `Demosaic::{Bilinear, MalvarHeCutler}`, to `u16` or `f32` (`Sample`); `demosaic`, `demosaic_cpu` and `demosaic_device`.
  - **`pattern`:** `test_pattern` and `psnr`.
- **`tetera-compute`:**
  - `kernels/bayer.cu` and `kernels/demosaic.cu`;
  - an `imaging` module: `Demosaic`, `MosaicLayout`, and `CudaBackend::{bayer_split, bayer_superpixel, demosaic}`.
- **The `demosaic` example.**
- **No new dependencies.**

## How the twins agree exactly

Both demosaics are linear filters whose weights, over a common power of two, are whole numbers: quarters for bilinear, sixteenths for Malvar–He–Cutler. The paper gives its filters in eighths, some with halves, so each is doubled. Every value is therefore an integer sum of sites:
- **in 16 bits,** the sum is rounded, halves up, and clamped to the mosaic's range;
- **as a float,** it is divided by the power of two. The sum is below 2²⁴, so the division is exact, and any overshoot below 0 or above the range is kept for later steps.

The twins are written two ways, so a slip in either shows:
- **the CPU** applies the paper's filters as tables of taps;
- **the kernel** computes the same sums, expanded.

The spec allows `f32` to differ by 1 least significant bit; it doesn't.

**Beyond the edges,** the mosaic is mirrored without repeating the edge: −1 reads 1. A site and its mirror are an even distance apart, so they have the same colour, and a flat colour comes through exactly to the last pixel.

## Ideas taken from OScope

Malvar–He–Cutler is from its paper (H. S. Malvar, L. He, R. Cutler, "High-quality linear interpolation for demosaicing of Bayer-patterned color images", ICASSP 2004), as the plan's Q22 chose.

OScope's P08 notes were read after this phase's design, and record the same choices, in its words:
- the edges mirrored about the outermost pixel, keeping every site's colour, checked with flat colours for every pattern;
- the CUDA twins in its compute crate, since its imaging crate may not use unsafe code;
- the superpixel dropping an odd last row or column;
- a chart with a grey zone plate, scored by PSNR.

**Where Tetera differs:**
- **Tetera demosaics the raw 16-bit sites in integers,** so the twins agree exactly. OScope demosaicked `f32` mosaics, so that black level and white balance could correct the mosaic first. That is P1.06's question for Tetera (see the follow-ups).
- **OScope's CPU Malvar–He–Cutler was faster:** 29.5 ms on 8 threads, against Tetera's 93 ms on 16, which mirrors every lookup.

## Design notes and deviations from the plan

- **The kernels are in `tetera-compute`,** not `tetera-imaging`:
  - **why:** launching a kernel is unsafe, and plan §5.3 allows unsafe code only in `tetera-compute` and a few others, not in `tetera-imaging`;
  - **the split:** `tetera-imaging` holds the image types, the CPU twins and the choice of backend; `tetera-compute` holds every kernel and its launch, behind safe functions that check sizes first;
  - **the plan:** §6.2's crate table now says so.
- **One `MosaicLayout`** describes a mosaic to both crates: its size, pattern and significant bits. The kernels' launches take it instead of six loose arguments.
- **The superpixel's green** is the mean of the tile's two greens, rounded halves up. The split and the superpixel leave out an odd last column or row, which has no tile of its own.
- **A mosaic needs at least 2 × 2 for bilinear, 3 × 3 for Malvar–He–Cutler,** for the mirror to stay inside it. Smaller ones are refused.
- **The example writes PPM,** not JPEG, which P1.05b adds. It writes each image's centre at most 960 × 640: a full frame in 16 bits is 120 MB.
- **The test pattern's zone plate** stops at an eighth of a cycle a pixel, half the mosaic's limit for red and blue, so it measures the methods rather than aliasing.

## Tests, and the mutants

- **35 new tests:** 28 in `tetera-imaging` (25 in every build, 3 more on the GPU), and 7 in `tetera-compute`'s `imaging` (2 in every build, 5 on the GPU). `tetera-compute` now has 33 tests without the feature, and 58 with it.
  - **Images:** a mosaic's checks; mirroring; crops moving the pattern; a raw frame, Bayer16 and Bayer8, becoming a mosaic; images and planes; their shape kept on the device.
  - **Bayer:** sampling RGB into each pattern; each plane's sites come from their own tile and colour, for every pattern; odd edges and mosaics without tiles; the superpixel's rounding, without overflow at 16 bits.
  - **Demosaic:**
    - every filter's weights sum to its denominator, and read only the colour it makes, or the site's own;
    - flat colours come through exactly to the edges, for every pattern, both methods, both types and three sizes;
    - a linear grey ramp comes through exactly, away from the edges;
    - a crop at an odd offset demosaics like the whole frame;
    - rounding and clamping in 16 bits, and exact overshoot as floats;
    - mosaics too small are refused;
    - Malvar–He–Cutler beats bilinear by more than 1 dB for every pattern.
  - **The pattern:** its bands, its zone plate finer outwards, its hues; pinned by its sums and three pixels; PSNR's arithmetic.
  - **The kernels, in `tetera-compute`:** hand-checked splits, superpixels and clamping; refusals of mismatched, undersized and oversized mosaics.
  - **The twins, on the GPU:** the split, the superpixel and both demosaics, to `u16` and `f32`, identical to the CPU at small sizes either side of a block for every pattern, and at full frame for RGGB. They pass with `nvcc`'s fat binary and with NVRTC.
- **Broken on purpose,** by hand:
  - **a crop that kept its pattern** failed 2 tests: the crop's own, and the odd-offset demosaic;
  - **edges that repeat** instead of mirroring failed 3: the mirror's own, the flat colours, and the PSNR ranking.
- **Mutants,** on both builds, for `tetera-imaging` and `tetera-compute`'s `imaging.rs`:
  - **the first runs missed, with CUDA,** 47 in `tetera-imaging` and 5 in `imaging.rs`:
    - the test pattern's arithmetic, which nothing pinned. It is pinned now: the PSNRs in these notes are against it;
    - `DeviceImage`'s size, and `split_cpu`'s guard against a mosaic one site wide, untested;
    - the launch-size check, unreachable behind the buffer checks: it comes first now, and is tested;
    - Malvar–He–Cutler at exactly its smallest height;
    - 7 equivalent, which no test can tell apart: six excluded by three entries in `.cargo/mutants.toml`, each with its reason (below), and one, `sum < 0` as `sum <= 0`, gone since the clamp became `sum.max(0)`.
  - **Without the feature, they also missed** every filter tap's sign, which only the twins checked: the linear ramp checks them on the CPU now. And `tiles()`'s test used sizes that hid two mutants.
  - **Three exclusions,** each with its reason:
    - a tile kernel's grid made larger, whose extra threads the kernel's bounds check idles;
    - `x − dx` for `x + dx` in `demosaic_cpu`, the same since every filter is point-symmetric;
    - `<=` for `<` at the test pattern's bands, which a pixel's centre never falls on.
  - **Now:**
    - `imaging.rs` with CUDA: 63 tested, 61 caught, 2 unviable;
    - `tetera-imaging` without the feature: 414 tested, 319 caught, 39 unviable. The 56 missed are code only the CUDA build compiles; the one that wasn't, the zone plate's radius, is now pinned (the pattern: 135 tested, 128 caught, 7 unviable);
    - with CUDA, `image.rs` was run again: 112 tested, 81 caught, 31 unviable. The crate's other misses were in code both builds compile, so the run without the feature covers them; the full CUDA run takes 35 minutes, and wasn't repeated.

## Follow-ups and known gaps

- **Binning, resize, the display transform and JPEG:** P1.05b.
- **Demosaicking a corrected mosaic:** P1.06's dark and flat correct the mosaic before it is demosaicked. If they give floats, demosaicking needs an `f32` input beside today's `u16`; the filters and their sums carry over.
- **The CPU demosaic** mirrors every lookup, interior or not. 93 ms a full frame is enough for a fallback; a faster interior loop is possible if the CPU path ever carries the live view.
- **Copies overlapped with work,** from P1.04b, if the preview pipeline (P1.14a) needs them.
