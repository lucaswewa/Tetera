## Summary

Tetera turns the field into raw frames: the MIChrome 20's sensor, with the stage moving during the exposure, on the GPU and the CPU. The phase's *Done when* holds:

- **The noise matches the model.** On an even field, green, 10 ms at a gain of 1:

  | Light, e⁻/ms | Mean, counts | Model | Variance | Model |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | 0 | 100.0 | 100.0 | 0.43 | 0.43 |
  | 1 | 101.6 | 101.7 | 0.83 | 0.86 |
  | 10 | 116.5 | 116.5 | 4.82 | 4.81 |
  | 100 | 264.9 | 264.9 | 44.10 | 44.28 |
  | 1,000 | 1,749.0 | 1,748.9 | 436.5 | 439.0 |
  | 2,200 | 3,727.8 | 3,727.7 | 959.8 | 965.2 |

  The model's variance is the photoelectrons', Poisson, plus the read noise's square, in counts, plus rounding's 1/12. The tests check each channel at five settings, and the dark current at three exposures and binnings.
- **A moving stage smears the field along its path:** a moving field's log is the mean of the still fields' logs along its path, within 1e-4, as Beer–Lambert's light should be. **A rolling shutter skews it by the line time:** a pixel every 40 rows at 10 µs a line, row 120 shifted exactly 3 pixels.
- **A frame repeats bit for bit from its seed** on each backend; the next frame's number, another seed, or any of the number's high bits gives other noise.
- **The two backends agree:** on a full 5472 × 3648 frame, 1 count of 19,961,856 differs between the GPU's sensor and the CPU's, by 1. The moving fields agree within 1e-4 of the light.
- **Faster on the GPU,** and P1.12a's one miss closed. The full field, against the 40 ms budget (§6.15):

  | Objective | P1.12a, still | P1.12a, moving | P1.12b, still | P1.12b, moving 100 pixels a frame |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | 5× | 9.3 ms | 9 to 36 ms | 4.8 ms | 4.8 to 31 ms |
  | 20× | 18.9 ms | 24 to 25 ms | 10.0 ms | 15.5 ms |
  | 50× | 38.6 ms | 41 to 46 ms | 24.8 ms | 26.7 to 32.8 ms |

  A raw frame at 20×, field and sensor, brought back to the host: 25 ms still, 31 ms jogging at 1 mm/s during a 10 ms exposure (16 samples a pixel).

## Ideas taken from OScope

The rolling shutter's line time comes from OScope's measured rate: 3,648 rows at 14.49 frames a second in 8 bits, 18.9 µs a line. The sensor's numbers are a 2.4 µm back-illuminated sensor's, typical rather than measured; P5.03 measures the real camera's.

## Performance

- **The resampling kernel was bound by double precision,** not by memory. It divided in double precision for each pixel and each path sample, and divided 64-bit integers for each texel; double precision runs at 1/64 of single on this GeForce GPU, and its division is a long sequence. With the pixels' map worked out on the host (four double multiply-adds a thread, no division), the path's samples in single precision from there, and each texel one 32-bit load:
  - still, the full field takes half its time at every objective;
  - jogging, a raw frame with 16 samples a pixel went from 207 ms to 31 ms.
- **The sensor's kernel takes 1.1 ms** for a full frame.
- **Bringing a raw frame back takes about 15 ms** of the 25: 40 MB into pageable memory. P1.12c's frames can come from pinned memory.
- **A moving frame's grid is wider** by the path's extent, so a jog's first frame makes the margin's tiles: 0.4 s at 20× the first time.
- **On the CPU,** a 912 × 608 raw frame takes 0.2 to 0.4 s; tests use 192 × 128 and smaller.

## Design notes and deviations from the plan

- **The field is light a 2.4 µm pixel's worth;** the sensor multiplies it by the binned area. A binned mode's field is the same field of view in bigger pixels (`Sensor::binned`), point-sampled at each binned pixel's middle: within 0.5 % of its four pixels' mean on average, 5 % at the sharpest edges.
- **Binning sums charge:** the light and the dark current scale with the binned area, the read noise with its side (b² reads add in quadrature), and the well with the area.
- **The noise is hashed, not streamed:** each pixel's draws come from the seed, its place, the frame's number and a salt, so any pixel can be drawn alone, in any order, on either backend. Poisson by inversion up to a mean of 16, a Gaussian of the same mean and variance above; Box–Muller for the Gaussians.
- **Only the well clips electrons;** read noise may take a dark pixel below its black level, as a real sensor's does, and the counts clip at 0 and 4,095.
- **3.76 e⁻ a count:** a full well at a gain of 1 is 4,089 counts with the black level, just within 12 bits. 8 bits are the top 8 of the 12.
- **The motion is constant over a frame:** a velocity, each row's exposure and the line time. The field's centre is where the stage was as row 0 began. P1.12c takes the velocity from `position_at` at a frame's start and end.
- **One sample for each pixel the stage moves during an exposure, at most 16;** after the blur, samples a pixel or two apart leave no trace of their spacing.
- **The GPU's sensor follows the CPU's order of operations,** with `__fmul_rn` and `__fadd_rn` so the compiler fuses nothing. The two differ only where single precision's `logf`, `cosf` or `expf` rounds a draw across a count's edge.
- **A texel's two stains are one 32-bit load,** the halves side by side as the tiles store them.

## Tests, and the mutants

- **18 new tests:**
  - **the sensor:** the RGGB mosaic; an exposure checked; the noise's mean and variance against the model at five settings; Poisson and Gaussian draws; the well and the bits; binning's light; the dark current with the exposure and the binning; a frame repeating from its seed and differing by its number, every high bit counting;
  - **motion:** its samples along each row's exposure; a moving field the mean of the still fields along its path; a rolling shutter's skew; a binned sensor's field;
  - **the stage:** where it will be, and was, during a frame;
  - **on the GPU, in `tetera-sim`:** the moving field's twins, three motions; the GPU's sensor against the CPU's at four exposures, repeating and differing by frame; a raw frame made on the GPU, against the CPU's, and binned through the same tiles;
  - **on the GPU, in `tetera-compute`:** a frame made, repeated, its high frame bits counted, and shapes refused.
- **Broken on purpose,** as the spec asks: **the noise model's variance,** the Gaussian shot noise's halved. Three tests caught it:
  - the noise against the model: a variance of 22.2 where the model gives 44.3;
  - the Poisson draws: a variance of 8.4 at a mean of 16.5;
  - the GPU's sensor against the CPU's: 34,313 counts apart, by up to 14.
- **Run 15 times,** both crates' tests passed each time.
- **Mutants,** of both crates, with CUDA on and the GPU tests enabled:
  - **the first runs missed 11:**
    - the dark current and the binned well, which no test drove: a test of the dark current with the exposure and the binning;
    - a frame number's high bits mixed in with `|` rather than `^`: frames apart only in a bit the salts share now differ;
    - the raw frame's buffer kept at the wrong size: a binned frame through the same tiles;
    - a frame number's high bits on the GPU: a frame 2⁴⁰ later differs;
    - the launch's checks on its sizes, now conversions, `i32::try_from`, with no comparison to mutate;
    - three equivalent, excluded with their reasons in `.cargo/mutants.toml`: the Poisson draw's boundary at a mean of exactly 16, its stop where a uniform draw equals the sum, and its cap of 64 counts, reached with probability about 1e-20.
  - **43 timed out** with four jobs sharing the GPU. Run again with two jobs and two minutes each, all but three were caught; those three make the work enormous: a grid's margin a thousand times too wide, or a Poisson draw that never ends.
  - **The last runs missed none:** 102 caught in `tetera-sim`, 109 in `tetera-compute` with the simulator's tests too, and all 23 in the resampling's launch with `tetera-compute`'s own tests alone. Two more misses there, the grid's offset within its first tile, gained a test of a grid starting at (−56, −30).
