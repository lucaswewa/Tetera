# P1.07c: Richardson–Lucy on the GPU

- **Status:** In review
- **Branch:** `m1/p1.07c-richardson-lucy-on-the-gpu`
- **Spec:** [phases.md](../phases.md#p107c-richardsonlucy-on-the-gpu); the tolerance is in [P1.07b's behaviour spec](P1.07b-psfs-and-richardson-lucy.md#the-backends-tolerance-for-p107c)
- **Size:** estimated 1,430 (code 655 · tests 655 · examples 90 · docs-as-code 15 · glue 15) → **measured 1,404** (code 715 · tests 576 · examples 104 · docs-as-code 4 · glue 5), **×0.98**

## Summary

Tetera now deconvolves on the GPU, in 2D and 3D, with total variation or without. The GPU matches the CPU well inside the tolerance P1.07b's behaviour spec set.
- **The tolerance:** after 20 iterations on 12-bit images, with random asymmetric PSFs, the backends differ by **4e-7 to 2.3e-6** in relative RMS, against the 1e-3 allowed. No pixel differs by more than **5e-5** of the image's largest value, against 1 %.
- **Each element-wise step matches its CPU twin bit for bit:** padding, cropping, the product with the PSF's transform, the ratio, the update and total variation. Only the FFTs differ, cuFFT's against `rustfft`'s.
- **At 20 MP,** 5,472 × 3,648 padded to 5,600 × 3,750, 20 iterations of 2D Richardson–Lucy take **339 to 373 ms** on the GPU over three runs, about 17.6 ms each, as the phase's *Done when* asks to record. On the 1,024 × 1,024 example an iteration takes 1.2 ms on the GPU against 25 ms on the CPU, and in 3D 3.0 ms against 94 ms.

## What was built

- **`deconvolve`** in `tetera-compute`:
  - `Padding`, the geometry both backends now share: the padded sizes, the image's offset, and where each PSF value goes with the centre at the origin. `reflect` and `smooth` moved here from `tetera-imaging`;
  - with the feature, `DeviceFft`, a cuFFT plan for complex transforms over a volume;
  - `deconvolve.cu`, and a safe launch for each step: `rl_pad`, `rl_crop`, `rl_to_complex`, `rl_multiply`, `rl_ratio`, `rl_update` and `rl_total_variation`.
- **`deconvolve`** in `tetera-imaging`:
  - `DeviceDeconvolution`, the CPU's `Deconvolution` step for step on the GPU, with the same floor and epsilon;
  - `richardson_lucy`, on whichever backend `Compute` chose;
  - the CPU's element-wise steps as named functions, each the twin of a kernel: `ratio`, `update`, `multiply`, `total_variation`, and the convolver's `pad` and `crop`.
- **The `deconvolve` example** runs each case on both backends, checks them against the tolerance, and times 20 iterations at 20 MP.
- **No new dependencies:** `cudarc`'s `cufft` feature has been on since P1.04a.

| 2D | 1,024 × 1,024, a 41 × 41 PSF | 24.6 ms an iteration | 1.23 ms | 7.5e-7 | 0.0032 |

**Setting up at 20 MP took 444 ms:** cuFFT's plan, the uploads, and the PSF's transform, from a host buffer of 168 MB. A pipeline that deconvolves frame after frame would plan once (P1.08).

**An iteration** is four transforms over the padded volume and six element-wise launches. At 20 MP the transforms take most of the time. Folding the conversion to complex into the update would save one pass over memory; it isn't needed yet.

## Design notes and deviations from the plan

- **One launch for each step,** rather than a fused kernel, as the second estimate planned. Each launch can then be checked against its CPU step alone, which is how they were made to match exactly.
- **Bit for bit, by rounding each operation alone.** nvcc fuses a multiply and an add into one instruction, rounded once, where Rust rounds twice. The kernels use the `_rn` intrinsics (`__fmul_rn`, `__fadd_rn`, `__fdiv_rn`, `__fsqrt_rn`), which nvcc never fuses, and order each sum as the CPU does. The behaviour spec's tolerance then only has to cover the FFTs' differences.
- **The PSF's transform is scaled as it is used.** The CPU scales its transform once, then multiplies. The GPU multiplies each value of cuFFT's transform by the scale inside `rl_multiply`, which rounds the same way, so the two products still match.
- **Out-of-place transforms:** `cudarc`'s safe `exec_c2c` takes its input and output as two buffers, so the iteration takes turns between two complex buffers.
- **One 3D plan for every volume:** cuFFT takes axes of 1, and the 20 MP image planned as one plane deep ran as fast as with a 2D plan, 16.9 ms an iteration against 17.3. The first version left the axes of 1 out and chose a 1D, 2D or 3D plan; the mutants showed no test could tell the two apart.
- **The padding's geometry moved to `tetera-compute`,** since that crate can't depend on `tetera-imaging`, and both backends must pad alike.
- **The GPU's memory:** at 20 MP the deconvolution holds about 750 MB: three complex buffers of 168 MB (the transform's two and the PSF's), three of floats, and cuFFT's workspace. It takes no `VramBudgets` grant yet; the pipeline engine (P1.08) shares out the GPU.

## Tests, and the mutants

- **9 new tests on the GPU, 2 in every build,** and 2 moved from `tetera-imaging` with the code they test:
  - **in `tetera-compute`'s `deconvolve`:**
    - `Padding`: its sizes, offsets and centring, and its refusals; with the tests of `reflect` and `smooth`, moved;
    - a point transformed to a flat spectrum and back, unnormalised, in 1D, 2D and 3D and for a single value; and a wave landing in its bin;
    - each kernel on a case worked by hand: a mirrored row, a complex product and its conjugate, the ratio at its floor, the update held at zero, total variation at a point;
    - each launch refusing each buffer of the wrong length.
  - **In `tetera-imaging`'s `deconvolve`, the twins:**
    - padding and cropping, exactly, at five sizes, among them a PSF wider than the image and a single pixel;
    - the ratio, the update with and without denominators, the product and its conjugate, and the conversion, exactly, over 10,007 values;
    - total variation, exactly, at four sizes, with flat patches and the hold at 0.1;
    - cuFFT against the CPU's transform at six sizes, forward and back, within 1e-5 of the largest value;
    - **the whole iteration** within the spec's tolerance: 20 iterations on blurred, noisy 12-bit specimens, 2D and 3D, plain and with total variation, each with a random PSF. The flux is kept on the device, and `richardson_lucy` takes the GPU;
    - the device refusing what the CPU refuses;
    - and, in every build, the floor and epsilon against the image's largest value.
  - `tetera-imaging` now has 100 tests without the feature, and 117 with it; `tetera-compute`, 38 and 80.
- **Broken on purpose,** as the spec asks: **the correction step's PSF left unmirrored,** the kernel ignoring its conjugate. It failed three tests: the whole iteration against the CPU, the product's twin, and the case worked by hand. The random PSFs are asymmetric for this purpose: a widefield PSF is its own mirror image, and would hide the break.
- **Mutants,** with CUDA, over this phase's changes:
  - **the first run:** in `tetera-compute`, 71 tested, 60 caught, 6 missed, 3 unviable, 2 timeouts; in `tetera-imaging`, 72 tested, 60 caught, 7 missed, 5 unviable. The CPU code they share runs in the CUDA build too.
  - **What they missed, and what changed:**
    - `DeviceFft`'s count and its `Debug`, which only `tetera-imaging`'s tests used: now tested where they are;
    - the 65,535-plane limit on total variation's grid, at its edge: now 65,535 passes and 65,536 is refused with its message;
    - the floor and epsilon, the same on both backends, so no twin could tell them wrong: now tested directly;
    - the device's flux, which the test only compared with itself: now checked to start where the CPU's does;
    - **leaving axes of 1 out of cuFFT's plan:** no test could tell it from planning them in, since cuFFT takes them. Timed at 20 MP, the two were alike, so every volume now takes one 3D plan, and the choice is gone.
  - **The second run:** `tetera-compute`'s 64 all caught, but 3 unviable and 2 timeouts; `tetera-imaging`'s 16 in the functions that had missed, all caught. Its full run takes 21 minutes, and wasn't repeated.
