## Summary

A raw frame now becomes a JPEG a browser can show. The chain bins the frame, demosaics it, resizes it to the preview's size, takes it through the display transform to 8 bits, and encodes it. Every step but the JPEG runs on the CPU or on CUDA, and the two agree exactly.

On a full frame, the whole chain to a 1920 × 1280 JPEG takes **43 ms on 16 CPU threads, and 7.7 ms with CUDA**, the JPEG on the CPU included. The JPEG alone takes **4.8 ms** at quality 80, inside the spec's 10 ms, through libjpeg-turbo.

## What was built

- **Binning** (`bayer::bin`, `bin_cpu`, `bin_device`; `MosaicLayout::binned`; `CudaBackend::bayer_bin`):
  - by 2, 3 or 4, by sum or by mean rounded halves up, keeping the Bayer layout;
  - each site of the result adds the `factor × factor` sites of its own colour in the block of tiles it stands for;
  - a sum adds the bits it needs (2 for 2 × 2, 4 for 3 × 3 and 4 × 4), and is refused beyond 16, with a pointer to the mean.
- **Resize** (`resize`: `ResizeAxis`, `Resize`, `resize`, `resize_cpu`, `resize_device`, `DeviceResize`; `CudaBackend::{upload_axis, resize}`):
  - area averaging to shrink, bilinear to enlarge, equal sizes copied;
  - each axis is a table of integer weights in 1/16384ths, made once for a pair of sizes;
  - a pixel's weights sum to 2²⁸, and it is rounded once;
  - the GPU gets the same tables, checked to stay inside the input when uploaded.
- **The display transform** (`display`: `Curve`, `Levels`, `DisplayTransform`, `display`, `display_cpu`, `display_device`, `DeviceDisplay`; `CudaBackend::display`):
  - black and white levels per channel, then a tone curve: linear, a gamma, or sRGB;
  - a lookup table of 65,536 entries per channel, made once and shared with the GPU.
- **JPEG** (`jpeg`: `JpegEncoder`, `encode`, `decode_rgb`): libjpeg-turbo through `turbojpeg` 1.5.1, RGB in 4:2:0 or grey. An encoder is made once and kept for a stream, MJPEG.
- **`tetera-compute`:** `resize.cu` and `display.cu`, binning in `bayer.cu`, and `DeviceElement`, so device images hold any element, bytes included.
- **The `demosaic` example** gains the preview chain, timed on each backend, and writes `preview.jpg`.
- **The doctor** checks `cmake` and `nasm`; the toolchain guide installs `nasm`.
- **Licences:** libjpeg-turbo's are in `licenses/libjpeg-turbo/`, with `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md` giving the IJG acknowledgement. `deny.toml` clarifies what `turbojpeg-sys` builds, and allows IJG for that crate alone, as the owner approved.
- **Dependencies:**
  - `turbojpeg` 1.5.1, `turbojpeg-sys` 1.2.0 (libjpeg-turbo 3.1.0), `cmake` 0.1.58;
  - pinned back to releases at least two weeks old: `cc` 1.11.2, `anyhow` 1.0.104, `find-msvc-tools` 0.1.15;
  - all locked crates are two weeks old or more.

## Performance

The live view's chain from a full frame, 5,472 × 3,648 in 12 bits, the best of 5, from `demosaic` in release:

| Step | CPU, 16 threads | CUDA |
|---|---|---|
| Bin 2 × 2, by the mean | 2.83 ms | 0.21 ms |
| Malvar–He–Cutler, 2,736 × 1,824 | 23.42 ms | 2.12 ms |
| Resize to 1,920 × 1,280 | 9.33 ms | 0.45 ms |
| The display transform, sRGB | 2.49 ms | 0.16 ms |
| JPEG at quality 80 | 4.76 ms, 348 KiB | on the CPU |
| **In all** | **42.82 ms** | **7.71 ms** |

- **The JPEG budget** (plan §6.15, 1920 × 1280 at quality 80, 10 ms or less) is met: 4.8 ms. The pure-Rust `jpeg-encoder` took 13.5 ms, which is why the owner chose libjpeg-turbo.
- **The CUDA column** times each step with its output's allocation. A full frame's Malvar–He–Cutler takes 0.74 ms in the kernel alone (P1.05a), so at a quarter of the size, most of the 2.1 ms is the allocation. The preview pipeline (P1.14a) keeps its buffers, and will show the kernels' own times.
- **Without the GPU,** the chain's 43 ms allows 23 previews a second: enough for a live view, though below the camera's fastest binned modes, up to 67 frames a second. The CPU is the fallback, not the live path.

## Design notes and deviations from the plan

- **Integer weights for resizing:**
  - **why:** floats on two backends round differently unless every multiply and add is written out the same, and an 8-bit preview would show a flipped rounding;
  - **how:** integer weights in tables made on the host, applied in 64-bit sums, agree exactly;
  - **the cost:** each weight is rounded to 1/16384, and its rounding's remainder goes to the largest weight, so every output's weights sum exactly to one.
- **One lookup table per channel** for the display transform. Levels and a curve become a table once, and applying it is a lookup on either backend. Per-channel levels leave room for white balance in P1.06.
- **Binning keeps the layout,** so a binned frame demosaics like any other. A sum grows the significant bits, as the camera's own binning would.
- **`DeviceElement`:** device images held only what demosaicking writes. They now hold any plain element, so the display transform's 8-bit output stays on the device.
- **JPEG by libjpeg-turbo,** the owner's choice, built from source with `cmake` and `nasm`. Its IJG licence is allowed for `turbojpeg-sys` alone, and the notices go with Tetera.
- **MJPEG is a stream of JPEGs from one encoder.** Its HTTP framing is P1.14b's.

## Tests, and the mutants

- **28 new tests:** 23 in `tetera-imaging` (21 in every build, 2 more on the GPU), and 5 in `tetera-compute`'s `imaging` (1 in every build, 4 on the GPU). `tetera-imaging` now has 46 tests without the feature, and 51 with it; `tetera-compute`, 34 and 63.
  - **Binning:**
    - each colour kept to itself, every pattern, factors 1 to 4, by sum and by mean;
    - hand-checked sums and means, a mean of 2.5 rounding up, and a sum refused beyond 16 bits;
    - **a property test:** binned sums keep each colour's total over the area binned, for random sizes, patterns, factors and values.
  - **Resize:**
    - every output's weights sum to one and stay inside the input, at ten pairs of sizes;
    - hand-computed tables for area averaging (6 → 2, 3 → 2, 7 → 3, 4 → 1) and bilinear (2 → 4, 3 → 5, 1 → 3, equal sizes);
    - flat images stay flat and ramps stay ramps, whatever the size;
    - each channel from its own values, on every row; a mean of 2.5 rounding up; the brightest value surviving rounding;
    - sizes that don't fit refused.
  - **The display transform:**
    - each table's ends are black and white, and it never falls, for every curve;
    - the curves' values, sRGB's 18 % grey among them;
    - each channel its own levels, and its own table, across the CPU's chunks;
    - bad levels, gammas and channel counts refused.
  - **JPEG:** a frame read back close, and closer at a higher quality; grey and odd sizes; one encoder for a stream; bad qualities, shapes and files refused.
  - **The kernels, in `tetera-compute`:** hand-checked binning, resizing over two rows, and table lookups; axes, resizes and transforms that don't fit refused.
  - **The twins, on the GPU:** binning for every pattern, factor and mode, at small sizes and full frame; resizing at five pairs of sizes, a full frame's preview among them; the display transform up to 1920 × 1280. All are identical, from `nvcc`'s fat binary and through NVRTC.
- **Broken on purpose:** binning that mixed the Bayer layout's colours, as the spec asks. `binning_keeps_each_colour_to_itself` gives each colour its own constant, so a bin that reads a neighbouring colour's site fails it. Broken by hand, a bin that read the next site instead of the next of its colour (`start + i`) failed 3 tests: that one, the hand-checked sums, and the property test. The mutants that move a bin's reads are all caught too.
- **Mutants,** on both builds, over this phase's changes (`--in-diff`):
  - **the first runs missed, with CUDA,** 7 in `tetera-imaging` and 8 in `tetera-compute`:
    - resize's weights: no test had a rounding remainder, unequal weights to give it to, or bilinear weights needing to round;
    - `ResizeAxis`'s sizes, unread by any test;
    - the launches' size checks, which every test passed with one row, or with one output row;
    - an axis's last window start, which the tests' sizes gave either way;
    - zero channels with nothing to do, accepted by `||` as `&&`.
  - **Without the feature, they also missed** the display transform's chunking, which only the GPU twins checked; and resizing with more than one channel, and rounding up, which only the twins checked.
  - **Tests were added for each.** 2 more, the area tables' spans made huge, time out, which counts as caught.
  - **Now:**
    - `tetera-imaging` without the feature: 262 tested, 204 caught, 39 unviable. The 19 missed are code only the CUDA build compiles, all caught with it;
    - `tetera-compute` with CUDA: 82 tested, 78 caught, 3 unviable. The one missed, an output count, has a test since, and the resize launch's 15 mutants are all caught;
    - no new exclusions.
