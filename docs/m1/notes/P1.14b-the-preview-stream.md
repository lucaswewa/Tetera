## Summary

The `preview` Thing streams the camera live over a WebSocket ([ADR-0019](../../adr/0019-the-preview-protocol.md)): each frame a header and a JPEG, for the viewport, region, quality and rate each client asks for, paced by its acknowledgements and lowered on a slow link. The camera Thing's stream now goes into the frame bus. The phase's *Done when* holds:

- **In mode 1 at 8 bits, a client that keeps up receives the camera's rate, at least 30 fps:** on the GPU, 50.7 frames a second over 4 s, at 1280 × 853.
- **From the exposure's end to a client's receipt takes 100 ms or less on localhost,** from the header's timestamps: 34 ms at the median, 36 ms at the 95th percentile.
- **A slow client never receives a stale frame, and its link steps down.** A test client that takes 150 ms over each frame gets frames that were each the newest when sent, at most 41 ms after their exposure, and its link steps down to quality 60. In the example, a client taking 200 ms comes down to quality 40.

## What was built

- **`tetera_acquire::protocol`,** the protocol's messages without the network:
  - **`Header`,** each frame's 64 bytes: the sequence number, the exposure's start and end on the instrument's clock, its end and the sending in UTC, the size, the quality and whether the link lowered it; `message` and `parse`, which skips a longer header from a later version;
  - **`ClientState`,** the client's whole state: viewport, region, quality, rate and the last frame painted. A field left out takes the defaults; a state no preview can take fails to deserialise, so teta-wot answers it with `{"error": …}`;
  - **`AckWindow`:** two frames at most sent and unacknowledged, each newer than the last;
  - **`LinkControl`:** a step down when the window held back more than half of the last second's previews, a step up after two seconds with less than a tenth held back; the steps cap the quality at 60, then 40, then scale the viewport to 0.75 and 0.5.
- **In `tetera-api`:**
  - **the `preview` Thing:**
    - `defaults`, a setting: what a client's preview starts from;
    - `sessions`: each open preview's parameters, link step, stats, frames sent, dropped and in flight, and its latency to the last acknowledgement;
    - `stream`, a binary stream: a session of P1.14a's for each client, the camera's white balance applied, with a one-preview slot between it and the socket;
  - **the camera's stream into the frame bus,** counted as before;
  - **the instrument's frame bus,** and where its previews are made: on the GPU when the camera renders there;
  - **the example `preview-stream`,** and the README's section on the stream.
- **ADR-0019,** the preview protocol.
- **No new dependencies:** `tokio-tungstenite` and `futures-util`, which the tests and the example use as a WebSocket client, were in the lock file already, through axum.

## How to try it

```bash
cargo run --release -p tetera-api --features cuda --example preview-stream
```

It serves the virtual instrument on a loopback port, streams the camera, and connects a quick client and a slow one, then prints each one's rate, its time from exposure to receipt, and how far its link came down. `-- --smoke` runs a small sensor on the CPU.

## Performance

- **A quick client on localhost gets the camera's rate:** 50.7 frames a second, against the camera's 51 over the same 4 s, with 34 ms from the exposure's end to the client's receipt. Most of it is P1.14a's preview, about 13 ms on the GPU at this size, and the frame's wait for the session.
- **A slow client costs the camera nothing.** Its session still makes previews at the camera's rate; the slot keeps the newest. The ones the window holds back are what step its link down.
- **The second of the two frames in flight is one paint old when read:** it waits in the socket while the client paints the first. A client taking 200 ms over each frame reads frames about 240 ms after their exposure, each of which was the newest when it was sent.
- **On the CPU** (the smoke run), the quick client got 22 frames a second at 87 ms: the CPU renders the camera's frames.

## Design notes and deviations from the plan

- **The stream is a teta-wot binary stream,** not a media route of Tetera's own as ADR-0017 expected: teta-wot keeps only the client's latest message, which suits a client that sends its whole state each time, acknowledgement and all. A lost message is made good by the next.
- **Both UTC times in the header go through the instrument clock's one mapping** (ADR-0006). In one test run, WSL2's wall clock stepped 3.3 s mid-stream, so a frame's age at sending, wall clock against the clock's mapping, read 3.4 s. Through the one mapping, their difference is the monotonic clock's. The server's latency in `sessions` is the monotonic clock's too.
- **A client's bad state changes nothing:** teta-wot keeps the last good one, so a preview goes on as it was.
- **A client that never acknowledges gets two frames,** then nothing, as the window says.
- **The latency is honest only on a real-time clock:** on a faster simulated clock, the clock's UTC runs ahead of the wall's.
- **MJPEG and the page moved to P1.14c** with the split, and Checkpoint B with them.

## Tests, and the mutants

- **8 new tests:**
  - **the protocol:** a header written and read back, a longer one skipped past, a bad one refused; a client's state over the defaults, bad ones refused; the window; the link's steps down and up, at their edges;
  - **the stream, over a real socket** with a WebSocket client: two frames until an acknowledgement, their headers and JPEGs; a slow client's frames, each the newest when sent, and its link stepping down; a client that says nothing getting the defaults and the camera's white balance; the client's state changing the preview, and bad states answered with errors; `sessions` while it runs and after;
  - **the camera's stream** reaching the bus, and the bus keeping no frame.
- **Broken on purpose,** as the spec asks:
  - **the acknowledgement window,** always open: three tests failed, the window's own, "no third before an acknowledgement", and the slow client, whose frames fell 213 behind the newest;
  - **the stale-frame check,** the slot keeping its oldest preview rather than the newest: the slow client's test failed, its frames 134 to 159 ms old when sent against 10 to 41 ms.
- **Run 15 times,** both crates' tests, 50 in all, passed each time.
- **Mutants:** 
  - **the first run,** over the change, 122 tested: 105 caught, 13 unviable, 1 timeout (the parameters compared the wrong way, so a session made again for every frame), and 3 missed:
    - the link's step up at its best, which no test held quick for two seconds there;
    - the `defaults` setting, which no test wrote;
    - the sessions' numbers starting at 1.
  - **Tests now cover them,** with no exclusions. **The last run,** over the three functions, 52 tested: 48 caught, 3 unviable, 1 timeout, **none missed**.

## Follow-ups and known gaps

- **MJPEG, the live statistics on the `preview` Thing, and the `live-view` page,** with Checkpoint B: P1.14c.
- **teta-wot's test client skips binary WebSocket messages,** so the stream's tests use `tokio-tungstenite` on a real socket. A `receive_binary` in teta-wot's `TestWebSocket` would let them run in-process; it would be an upstream U-phase (ADR-0004) if more streams need it.
- **Two clients of the same size do the work twice.** A shared session for equal parameters can wait until there are many clients.
