## Summary

A raw frame now becomes a corrected, colour-balanced preview: the ISP, stage by stage, on the CPU or on CUDA. The two backends agree exactly at every stage and over the whole chain. That chain is P1.06b's reference for the fused kernel.

A synthetic sensor with an offset, vignetting and unequal channels shows it working. Through the ISP with that sensor's own dark, flat field and white balance, the test pattern comes back even and neutral. An even field comes back flat within 1 %, as the spec asks.

From a 2 × 2 binned frame to 1920 × 1280, the chain takes **3.1 ms with CUDA** and 42 ms on 16 CPU threads. From a full 20 MP frame, 9.7 ms and 112 ms.

## What was built

- **`correct`** in `tetera-imaging`:
  - `MasterDark` and `DarkLibrary`: a master dark chosen by the frame's mode, binning, gain and size, and an exposure within 2 % of the frame's, the nearest if several;
  - dark subtraction, clamped at zero (`subtract_dark`, `_cpu`, `_device`);
  - `FlatField`: from a dark-subtracted master flat, a gain per site, its Bayer channel's mean over its own value, in 1/4096ths. A site below 5 % of its channel's mean keeps a gain of 1. Applied rounded and clamped to the mosaic's range (`apply_flat`, `_cpu`, `_device`).
- **`colour`:**
  - `ColourCorrection`, white balance and a 3 × 3 matrix, combined into one fixed-point matrix in 1/16384ths;
  - `Colour`, that matrix with levels and a transfer curve as tables;
  - `colour`, `_cpu` and `_device`: each pixel through the matrix, rounded once and clamped to 16 bits, then each channel through its table to 8 bits.
- **`isp`:**
  - `Reduce` (the whole frame, a crop, or a bin by the mean);
  - `IspParams`, the calibration inputs in one struct;
  - `Isp`, the chain prepared for one raw layout, its calibrations checked against the reduced layout;
  - `DeviceIsp`, its tables on the GPU;
  - `run`, `run_cpu`, and `DeviceIsp::run`.
- **`pattern::Sensor`:** a synthetic, noise-free sensor: offset, vignetting, a response per channel; it exposes a linear image as a raw mosaic, and gives its own dark.
- **`tetera-compute`:** `isp.cu` (dark subtraction, the flat field, colour) and their launches in an `isp` module.
- **The `isp` example:** the chain on each backend, from a full frame and from a binned one, each stage timed, the previews compared and written as JPEGs.
- **No new dependencies.**

## The chain, and its order

crop or bin → dark → flat → demosaic → **resize → white balance and CCM → levels and curve → 8 bits**.

**It resizes before colour, in linear light.** Plan D7 and the spec put resizing last, on 8-bit values. Resizing and the colour matrix are both linear, so they commute but for rounding. Moving the resize earlier gives three things:
- **less work:** colour and its table lookups apply to the preview's 2.5 MP, not the frame's 5 or 20 MP;
- **a better image:** averaging in linear light is correct, while averaging gamma-encoded values darkens fine detail;
- **a lighter fused kernel:** P1.06b can apply colour once per output pixel, not once per resize tap.

D7 and P1.06b's spec now give this order.

**Every stage is integer arithmetic,** as in P1.05a and P1.05b:
- the flat field's gains are 1/4096ths;
- the colour matrix is 1/16384ths;
- the curve is a table.

So the twins agree exactly, and the fused kernel can be held to the same results.

## Ideas taken from OScope

From its documents, in our own words (ADR-0002): OScope's P08 notes record correcting the mosaic before demosaicking, so that black level and white balance act on the sensor's own values. Tetera does that with integers on the mosaic, where OScope used floats (see P1.05a's notes).

Master darks matched by exposure and gain, flat fields normalised per channel, and colour matrices into linear sRGB are standard practice.

## Design notes and deviations from the plan

- **Resizing before colour,** as above.
- **Dark matching:**
  - the gain, mode, binning and size must match exactly, and the exposure within 2 % of the frame's: dark current grows with the exposure, and 2 % of it is below the sensor's noise;
  - with no match, the error names what was asked for, so the operator can take the missing dark (P1.18).
- **The flat field's guard:**
  - a site below 5 % of its channel's mean, dust or a dead pixel, keeps a gain of 1, rather than one that would multiply its noise without bound;
  - gains are capped just under 16, the most a fixed-point gain holds;
  - a flat whose channel averages below 1 is refused.
- **Clamping:**
  - the flat field clamps to the mosaic's range, so a saturated site stays saturated;
  - colour clamps to 0 and 65,535 before its tables, so a negative mix is black.
- **Calibrations at the reduced layout:** a binned or cropped chain takes masters made for that layout. A camera binning in hardware gives frames in that mode, and its darks are taken in the same mode.
- **On CUDA, a crop is made on the host** before the upload, which also uploads less. Binning runs on the device.
