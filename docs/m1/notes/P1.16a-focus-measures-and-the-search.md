## Summary

Autofocus's two halves that don't need the instrument: four focus measures on a raw frame's green channel, and a search through z that finds their peak against backlash. The measures are in `tetera-imaging`, the search in `tetera-workflows`, over a probe it drives, which P1.16b makes of the instrument. The phase's *Done when* holds:

- **The spec came first,** committed before the code; two of its choices were changed after the first runs, each change marked in it.
- **Each measure peaks at a synthetic stack's focus,** in the tests, and at the section's middle on the virtual camera, the simulator's true focus, in the example.
- **On known curves with noise, through a probe with backlash and steps, the search's error is a quarter of the depth of field or less in 100 of 100 trials** at each objective's depth of field, on peaks from 0.75 to 6 depths of field wide; the tests ask 99.
- **A flat curve fails cleanly,** the probe back where it started, approached from below.

## Behaviour spec

Written before the code, as a spec-first phase's must be. It covers P1.16b's use of the search on the instrument too.

### What autofocus finds

The stage's z where the image is sharpest: the peak of a **focus measure**, a number that grows as the image sharpens, taken through z. In brightfield, a defocused image is the in-focus one blurred, so its fine detail, its gradients and its variance fall away from focus on both sides.

### Focus measures

Each is taken on **the raw frame's green channel, binned**:

- **The green channel:** each whole 2 × 2 Bayer cell gives one value, the mean of its two green samples. Green has the most samples, the sensor's best response, and H&E's strongest contrast in transmitted light. No demosaic is needed. The camera gives raw Bayer frames only, so grey ones aren't taken.
- **Binned** by the least whole number k that brings it to at most 512 values across, each value the mean of a k × k block, for speed and to average the sensor's noise. A part block at the right or the bottom is left out, and at least 3 × 3 values must remain. The live mode's 2736 × 1824 frame gives 1368 × 912 cells, binned 3 × 3 to 456 × 304.

On that image G, W × H values with mean μ, four measures, each higher when sharper:

- **Brenner's gradient** (J. F. Brenner and others, "An automated microscope for cytologic research", J. Histochem. Cytochem. 24, 1976): the mean over the image of `(G[x+2, y] − G[x, y])²`.
- **The variance of the Laplacian** (J. L. Pech-Pacheco and others, "Diatom autofocusing in brightfield microscopy: a comparative study", ICPR 2000): the variance, over the interior, of `G[x−1, y] + G[x+1, y] + G[x, y−1] + G[x, y+1] − 4 G[x, y]`.
- **Normalised variance** (F. C. A. Groen, I. T. Young and G. Ligthart, "A comparison of different focus functions for use in autofocus algorithms", Cytometry 6, 1985): `Σ (G − μ)² / (N μ)`, N = W H; 0 when μ is 0 or below. Dividing by μ tempers the light's effect: the variance grows as the square of the brightness, normalised variance only in proportion.
- **Tenengrad** (J. M. Tenenbaum, Stanford, 1970; E. Krotkov, "Focusing", IJCV 1, 1987): the mean, over the interior, of `Gx² + Gy²`, Gx and Gy the Sobel operator's. Without a threshold: Groen and others found thresholded measures' results depend on an arbitrary threshold.

**The default is normalised variance.** Groen, Young and Ligthart name it among the best of the eleven they compared. Yu Sun, S. Duthaler and B. J. Nelson ("Autofocusing in computer microscopy: selecting the optimal focus algorithm", Microsc. Res. Tech. 65, 2004) compared eighteen on 139,000 brightfield, phase and DIC images, and found normalised variance the best overall without preprocessing. They found gradient measures better on subsampled images; Tetera's images are binned, which averages, rather than subsampled, which drops. P1.16b's example runs every measure on the simulator, so the choice is checked against it.

### The depth of field

The search's range and steps are set by the objective's depth of field: `d = λ / NA² + e / (M · NA)`, λ = 0.55 µm, e the sensor's pixel pitch, M and NA the objective's (the formula Nikon and Olympus give, and the one the simulator uses, P1.12a). With the MIChrome 20's 2.4 µm pixels: 5×, NA 0.14: 31.5 µm; 10×, NA 0.28: 7.9 µm; 20×, NA 0.42: 3.4 µm; 50×, NA 0.55: 1.9 µm.

### The search

From the stage's z when it starts, z₀:

1. **The coarse sweep:** from `z₀ − R` to `z₀ + R` in steps of d, R = 8 d by default: 17 positions. Each is measured.
2. **The coarse peak** is the highest position, z_c.
3. **The fine sweep:** from `z_c − 2 d` to `z_c + 2 d` in steps of d / 2: 9 positions. *Widened from ±d in quarters after the first runs: a peak 4 to 6 depths of field wide, with 0.2 % noise, falls only 1 % within ±d, and the narrower sweep found it within d / 4 in 389 of 400 trials; the wider one, in 400 of 400, and narrow peaks still in 400 of 400.*
4. **The fit:** a parabola, by least squares, through the logarithm of the measure at the fine sweep's positions around its highest that lie in its top half, above the midpoint of the sweep's highest and lowest measures, and at least three: a Gaussian peak's logarithm is a parabola, so the fit is exact for one. A narrow peak gives three to five positions, a broad one all nine, so its flat top has curvature enough to fit. If any of them is 0 or below, the measure itself is fitted. *The top half was chosen after the first runs: the highest and two either side, as first written, found a peak 5 depths of field wide within d / 4 in only 97 of 100.* Its vertex is the focus, if the parabola opens downward and its vertex lies among those positions; else the highest position is. Each position is where the stage reported itself, so its steps count, not where it was asked to go.
5. **The move to focus,** and the result: the focus, the measure used, and the curve, each position and its measure in the order taken, the coarse sweep's and the fine sweep's marked.

**Always from below, against backlash.** A lead screw's backlash puts the stage in a different place for the same command, depending on the direction it came from. Every position is reached moving up: before each sweep, and before the move to focus, the stage goes down to a take-up distance b below the first position, 10 µm by default, then up. Within a sweep it only moves up. A backlash smaller than b then offsets every position by the same amount, measured and final alike, and the focus found is where the stage returns to.

**The range is the screw's.** On the way up, the carriage rides up to the backlash below the screw, so the sweep sees the sample from about `z₀ − R − backlash` to `z₀ + R − backlash`. A focus within ±6 d of the start then leaves room for a backlash of about 1.5 d: 2.9 µm at 50×. *Added after the first tests, which assumed 4 µm at 50×.*

The search refuses a range that doesn't fit the stage's z travel, before it moves: from the coarse sweep's first position, less the fine sweep's reach and the take-up, to its last, plus the fine sweep's reach.

### No focus

The search finds no focus, and moves the stage back to z₀, from below, when:

- **the curve is flat:** the coarse sweep's highest measure is less than 5 % above its median. A blank field gives the light's unevenness and the sensor's noise, which don't change with z;
- **the peak is at the coarse sweep's end,** first or last: the focus may lie beyond the range.

It says which, with the curve's contrast. A job fails with that error, `not_found`, and logs the curve.

### Frames after the stage stops (P1.16b)

On the instrument, each measure comes from **the first frame whose exposure started after the stage reported itself still** at that position, on the instrument's clock, so a frame exposed while the stage moved never counts. The live mode's frames (D5) are used as they stream: no mode switch.

### What P1.16b checks on the simulator

The simulator's section is in focus at the stage's z of `section_z` to `section_z + thickness`, and the true focus is its middle. At 5× and at 50×, 100 starts at random distances within ±6 d of it: the focus found is within d / 4 of it in at least 95. At 10×, a search takes 5 s or less. With the sample unloaded, it finds no focus and leaves the stage where it was.

### How long it takes

At 10×, 26 positions and three take-ups. A move of d, 7.9 µm, at the z axis's 2 mm/s and 20 mm/s², takes about 40 ms, and the stage reports it still within 25 ms. A frame exposed after that comes within one of the live mode's 19 ms intervals and its 10 ms exposure. With the measure's few milliseconds, each position takes about 90 ms: about 2.5 s in all.

## What was built

- **In `tetera-imaging`, `focus`:** `green`, a mosaic's green channel binned to at most 512 across; `FocusMeasure`, Brenner's gradient, the variance of the Laplacian, normalised variance (the default) and Tenengrad, each `of` a grey image or `of_mosaic`; `Grey`, the image they take.
- **In `tetera-workflows`, `focus`:**
  - **`search(probe, depth_of_field, limits, options, step)`:** the coarse sweep, the fine one, the fit and the move to focus, every position from below; no focus on a flat curve or a peak at the range's end, back at the start; a range beyond the stage's travel refused before a move;
  - **`FocusProbe`,** what it drives: the stage's z, a move that ends when the stage is still, and a measure;
  - **`SearchOptions`,** in depths of field, and **`Focus`,** the result: where, from where, the contrast, and the curve; `found` gives the focus or a `not_found` error;
  - **`vertex`,** the fit, on its own;
  - **`focus::synthetic::Synthetic`,** a stage whose carriage lags its screw by its backlash and moves in whole steps, over a known curve with noise, for tests and examples;
  - **the example `focus-search`**.
- **No new dependencies;** `tetera-imaging` takes `serde` and `schemars`, already the workspace's, for the measure's name.

## Performance

- **The measures on the simulator,** H&E at 20× on a 384 × 256 sensor, from 8 depths of field away to focus: Tenengrad rises 19.9 times, Brenner 10.7, normalised variance 2.5, and the variance of the Laplacian only 1.3, the sensor's noise in its second differences. All four peak at the section's middle, to the stack's step.
- **Their widths differ as much:** Brenner's and Tenengrad's peaks are about a depth of field wide; normalised variance's, about two, with a flat top that falls 6 % within ±d. The search's fine sweep and fit were widened for that (see the spec).
- **The search takes 26 positions** at every objective: 17 coarse, 9 fine. The instrument's time for each is P1.16b's to measure.
- **Its error, 100 trials at each objective,** with 2 µm of backlash and the virtual stage's 0.3125 µm steps: on peaks 0.75 to 1.25 d wide with 1 % noise, the median 0.01 to 0.04 d and the worst 0.04 to 0.11 d; on peaks 4 to 6 d wide with 0.2 % noise, the median 0.01 to 0.04 d and the worst 0.09 to 0.15 d. At 50× the stage's step, 0.16 d, is most of it.

## Design notes and deviations from the plan

- **The default stays normalised variance,** as the literature has it, though it is the flattest of the three that work on the simulator. P1.16b's 100 trials on the virtual instrument decide whether it, or a gradient measure, is the default there.
- **Two of the spec's choices were changed after the first runs,** each marked where it stands:
  - **the fit's points:** the top half of the fine sweep's peak, not the highest and two either side, so a broad peak's flat top has curvature enough to fit;
  - **the fine sweep:** ±2 d in halves, not ±d in quarters, for the same nine positions. A peak 5 d wide with 0.2 % noise was found within d / 4 in 389 of 400 trials before, 400 of 400 after, and narrow peaks in 400 of 400 either way.
- **The range is the screw's,** a note added to the spec: the backlash shifts the window the carriage sees by its own size. Starts within ±6 d of focus leave room for 1.5 d of it; the first tests assumed 4 µm at 50×, 2.1 d, and found peaks at the range's end.
- **The code was drafted before its estimate was committed,** while P1.15d's mutants ran; the estimate and the split had been written first, in the split's script, and weren't changed.
- **Each position is where the stage reported itself,** so the fit takes the stage's steps into account rather than where it was asked to go.
- **The probe is a trait the search drives,** not a stage and a camera, so the search is tested on synthetic curves of every shape, and P1.16b only adds the instrument's.
- **Two of P1.15a's tests were fixed here,** as the gate's run found them flaky under load: the compute pool's three jobs had to all start within one's 60 ms, and now wait for the pool to fill; and the history, written just after a change in memory, is now waited for.

## Tests, and the mutants

- **17 new tests:**
  - **the measures:** each one's value on images whose answer is known, a ramp, a plane, a checkerboard, a parabola across and down; a flat image 0; the green channel for each Bayer pattern; binning to fit, a part block left out, and too small refused; each measure's peak through a synthetic stack, falling away on both sides; a mosaic's red and blue not counted;
  - **the search:** 100 trials on narrow peaks with 1 % noise and 100 on broad ones with 0.2 %, through backlash and steps, at least 99 within d / 4; the take-up before each sweep and the focus, every measure from below; a flat curve, a contrast just under and just over the least, and at exactly the least; a peak at either end, and just inside; bad options and a range beyond the travel, refused before a move; the step's progress, and its error stopping the search; the fit's vertex, its window, and its 3 × 3 solve; the synthetic stage itself, pinned to SplitMix64's first output.
- **Broken on purpose,** as the spec asks:
  - **the approach direction,** each position and the focus approached from above: three tests failed, the stage ending 6 µm, its backlash, from focus;
  - **the flat-curve check:** a blank curve's focus "found", 1,487.5 µm.
- **Run 15 times:** the two crates' tests, `tetera-imaging`'s and `tetera-workflows`', 124 in all, passed 15 of 15.
- **Mutants:**
  - **the first run,** over the change, 578 tested: 535 caught, 2 unviable, 4 timeouts, **37 missed.** Test images and depths whose numbers coincided, 4 × 4 with `h − 2 = h / 2`, a depth of 2 with `2 · 2 = 2 + 2`, a median of 1; the fit's window, which exact Gaussians don't need; the contrast at exactly the least; and the synthetic stage's bit mixing.
  - **Tests now cover them:** the window tested on its own, sizes and depths that don't coincide, the contrast over a base of 2, the stage pinned to SplitMix64's known output. One exclusion: the Laplacian's sum negated, as only its square counts.
  - **The last runs** missed none. Four time out: the window's step turned to `/= 1` or `*= 1` never ends.
