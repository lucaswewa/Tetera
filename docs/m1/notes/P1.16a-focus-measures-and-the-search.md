### What autofocus finds

The stage's z where the image is sharpest: the peak of a **focus measure**, a number that grows as the image sharpens, taken through z. In brightfield, a defocused image is the in-focus one blurred, so its fine detail, its gradients and its variance fall away from focus on both sides.

### Focus measures

Each is taken on **the raw frame's green channel, binned**:

- **The green channel:** each whole 2 × 2 Bayer cell gives one value, the mean of its two green samples. Green has the most samples, the sensor's best response, and H&E's strongest contrast in transmitted light. No demosaic is needed. The camera gives raw Bayer frames only, so grey ones aren't taken.
- **Binned** by the least whole number k that brings it to at most 512 values across, each value the mean of a k × k block, for speed and to average the sensor's noise. A part block at the right or the bottom is left out, and at least 3 × 3 values must remain. The live mode's 2736 × 1824 frame gives 1368 × 912 cells, binned 3 × 3 to 456 × 304.

On that image G, W × H values with mean μ, four measures, each higher when sharper:

- **Brenner's gradient** (J. F. Brenner and others, "An automated microscope for cytologic research", J. Histochem. Cytochem. 24, 1976): the mean over the image of `(G[x+2, y] − G[x, y])²`.
- **The variance of the Laplacian** (J. L. Pech-Pacheco and others, "Diatom autofocusing in brightfield microscopy: a comparative study", ICPR 2000): the variance, over the interior, of `G[x−1, y] + G[x+1, y] + G[x, y−1] + G[x, y+1] − 4 G[x, y]`.
- **Normalised variance** (F. C. A. Groen, I. T. Young and G. Ligthart, "A comparison of different focus functions for use in autofocus algorithms", Cytometry 6, 1985): `Σ (G − μ)² / (N μ)`, N = W H; 0 when μ is 0 or below. Dividing by μ tempers the light's effect: the variance grows as the square of the brightness, normalised variance only in proportion.
- **Tenengrad** (J. M. Tenenbaum, Stanford, 1970; E. Krotkov, "Focusing", IJCV 1, 1987): the mean, over the interior, of `Gx² + Gy²`, Gx and Gy the Sobel operator's. Without a threshold: Groen and others found thresholded measures' results depend on an arbitrary threshold.

**The default is normalised variance.** Groen, Young and Ligthart name it among the best of the eleven they compared. Yu Sun, S. Duthaler and B. J. Nelson ("Autofocusing in computer microscopy: selecting the optimal focus algorithm", Microsc. Res. Tech. 65, 2004) compared eighteen on 139,000 brightfield, phase and DIC images, and found normalised variance the best overall without preprocessing. They found gradient measures better on subsampled images; Tetera's images are binned, which averages, rather than subsampled, which drops. P1.16b's example runs every measure on the simulator, so the choice is checked against it.

### The depth of field

The search's range and steps are set by the objective's depth of field: `d = λ / NA² + e / (M · NA)`, λ = 0.55 µm, e the sensor's pixel pitch, M and NA the objective's (the formula Nikon and Olympus give, and the one the simulator uses, P1.12a). With the MIChrome 20's 2.4 µm pixels: 5×, NA 0.14: 31.5 µm; 10×, NA 0.28: 7.9 µm; 20×, NA 0.42: 3.4 µm; 50×, NA 0.55: 1.9 µm.

### The search

From the stage's z when it starts, z₀:

1. **The coarse sweep:** from `z₀ − R` to `z₀ + R` in steps of d, R = 8 d by default: 17 positions. Each is measured.
2. **The coarse peak** is the highest position, z_c.
3. **The fine sweep:** from `z_c − d` to `z_c + d` in steps of d / 4: 9 positions.
4. **The fit:** a parabola, by least squares, through the logarithm of the measure at the fine sweep's highest position and up to two on each side: a Gaussian peak's logarithm is a parabola, so the fit is exact for one. If any of them is 0 or below, the measure itself is fitted. Its vertex is the focus, if the parabola opens downward and its vertex lies among those positions; else the highest position is. Each position is where the stage reported itself, so its steps count, not where it was asked to go.
5. **The move to focus,** and the result: the focus, the measure used, and the curve, each position and its measure in the order taken, the coarse sweep's and the fine sweep's marked.

**Always from below, against backlash.** A lead screw's backlash puts the stage in a different place for the same command, depending on the direction it came from. Every position is reached moving up: before each sweep, and before the move to focus, the stage goes down to a take-up distance b below the first position, 10 µm by default, then up. Within a sweep it only moves up. A backlash smaller than b then offsets every position by the same amount, measured and final alike, and the focus found is where the stage returns to.

The search refuses a range that doesn't fit the stage's z travel, before it moves: from the coarse sweep's first position, less the fine sweep's reach and the take-up, to its last, plus the fine sweep's reach.

### No focus

The search finds no focus, and moves the stage back to z₀, from below, when:

- **the curve is flat:** the coarse sweep's highest measure is less than 5 % above its median. A blank field gives the light's unevenness and the sensor's noise, which don't change with z;
- **the peak is at the coarse sweep's end,** first or last: the focus may lie beyond the range.

It says which, with the curve's contrast. A job fails with that error, `not_found`, and logs the curve.

### Frames after the stage stops (P1.16b)

On the instrument, each measure comes from **the first frame whose exposure started after the stage reported itself still** at that position, on the instrument's clock, so a frame exposed while the stage moved never counts. The live mode's frames (D5) are used as they stream: no mode switch.

### What P1.16b checks on the simulator

The simulator's section is in focus at the stage's z of `section_z` to `section_z + thickness`, and the true focus is its middle. At 5× and at 50×, 100 starts at random distances within ±6 d of it: the focus found is within d / 4 of it in at least 95. At 10×, a search takes 5 s or less. With the sample unloaded, it finds no focus and leaves the stage where it was.

### How long it takes

At 10×, 26 positions and three take-ups. A move of d, 7.9 µm, at the z axis's 2 mm/s and 20 mm/s², takes about 40 ms, and the stage reports it still within 25 ms. A frame exposed after that comes within one of the live mode's 19 ms intervals and its 10 ms exposure. With the measure's few milliseconds, each position takes about 90 ms: about 2.5 s in all.
