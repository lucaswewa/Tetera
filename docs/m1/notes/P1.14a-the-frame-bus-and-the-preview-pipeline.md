## Summary

`tetera-acquire`, a new L4 crate, takes every frame from the camera to whatever needs it, and turns frames into preview JPEGs and live statistics, before any network is involved. The phase's *Done when* holds:

- **In mode 1 at 8 bits, each session keeps up with the camera's rate, at least 30 fps, and each stage's time is recorded.** On the GPU, the camera ran at 50.6 fps over 3 s, start-up included (its fastest is 53.1), and both sessions, 1920 × 1280 and 640 × 427, made 53.0 and 53.2 previews a second over the last second, one frame each giving way. Each preview carries its stages' times, and each session their means.
- **The statistics match a reference computed from the raw frame:** a plain, site-by-site reference, for three Bayer patterns, 8 and 12 bits, a region, a mask of odd size, and a row and column of their own.
- **A latest-wins subscriber drops frames when slow; a lossless one slows its publisher instead.** A reader taking 20 ms a frame, fed every 2 ms, sees fewer than 20 of 50 frames, the last among them, and holds up nothing. A lossless reader taking 10 ms a frame gets all 20, in order, and holds its publisher for 150 ms or more.

## What was built

- **`crates/tetera-acquire`,** L4, on `tetera-core`, `tetera-compute`, `tetera-imaging`, `tetera-pipeline` and `tetera-hal`:
  - **`FrameBus`** (ADR-0008), the HAL's `FrameSink`, so a camera streams into it:
    - `lossless(capacity)`: every frame, in order, from a bounded queue; a full queue holds the publisher until it has room, and `waits()` counts the times it did;
    - `latest()`: the newest frame not yet taken, from a one-frame mailbox, which counts the frames `replaced()`;
    - `first_after(t, timeout)`: the first frame whose exposure starts at `t` or later;
    - subscribers held weakly: a dropped one is let go at the next frame, and a dropped lossless one frees a publisher waiting on it.
  - **`Session`,** a preview session, on a thread of its own:
    - `PreviewParams`: the viewport, the region, the JPEG's quality, the maximum rate and the white balance, checked, and changed mid-stream with `set_params`;
    - `Link`, the slow link's hook for P1.14b: a cap on the quality and a scale on the viewport;
    - `fit` keeps the region's shape within the viewport; `binning` picks the largest of 1 to 4 that leaves the region at least the preview's size; `recipe` builds the live recipe, `[bin →] demosaic → resize → colour`, which the pipeline fuses into one kernel on the GPU (P1.08c);
    - each `Preview` carries its frame's metadata, its size, the quality it was encoded at, the JPEG, and each stage's time;
    - `SessionStats`: previews made, frames given way, failures, and the rate and each stage's mean over the last second; `last_error`.
  - **`statistics`,** a frame's live statistics from its raw mosaic, in parallel bands of rows: a histogram for each colour, the saturation mask at the preview's size and the share of sites saturated, profiles along a row and down a column cell by cell, each colour's statistics in a region, and Brenner's focus measure on green.
  - **`LiveStats`** works them out on a thread of its own, from a latest-wins subscriber, at most 10 times a second.
- **The binned live mode by default (D5):** the instrument configures the camera to mode 1, 2 × 2 binned, at 8 bits, when it starts (`tetera-api`'s `LIVE_MODE`).
- **The example `preview-pipeline`;** the crate's README.
- **No new dependencies:** `rayon`, `schemars` and `serde` were in the workspace.

## How to try it

```bash
cargo run --release -p tetera-acquire --features cuda --example preview-pipeline
```

It streams the virtual camera in its binned live mode into the bus for 3 s, the stage jogging, with two preview sessions, 1920 × 1280 and 640 × 427, and the live statistics. It prints each one's rate and each stage's time, and writes a few previews to `target/preview-pipeline/`. `-- --smoke` runs a small sensor on the CPU for a second.

## Performance

- **On the GPU, a 1920 × 1280 preview takes 12.7 ms:** the frame to a mosaic 1.6 ms, the live recipe 7.6 ms (bin, demosaic, resize and colour, with the copies to and from the GPU), the JPEG 3.4 ms. A session could make about 78 a second, so it keeps up with the camera's 53 with room to spare; two sessions share the GPU and still both keep up.
- **At 640 × 427,** 9.1 ms: the JPEG is 0.4 ms, and the recipe, which reads the whole binned frame, is most of it.
- **A JPEG at quality 80** is 42 KiB at 1920 × 1280 over glass, and about 130 KiB with tissue across half the field: up to 7 MB/s at 53 a second, for P1.14b's slow links to lower.
- **The statistics come 8.7 times a second,** at most 10: after 100 ms, the thread waits for the next frame, up to 19 ms at 53 fps. They are worked out on the CPU over the binned frame, 2736 × 1824, within that time.

## Design notes and deviations from the plan

- **The bus keeps no frame.** A latest-wins mailbox holds one until it is taken or replaced, and a lossless queue its capacity; once its readers let a frame go, its buffer goes back to the camera's pool. So a slow lossless reader can run the pool dry, which is the backpressure the recorder needs (P1.15b), and nothing else can.
- **A session's maximum rate waits, then takes the newest frame.** A first version took a frame and dropped it if it came too soon, so the last frame of a burst could be lost. Now the frames that come while a session waits give way to the newest, and the last one is always shown.
- **Binning comes before the recipe, chosen by size:** the largest of 1 to 4 that leaves the region at least the preview's size, so the recipe's resize only ever shrinks a little, and less data goes to the GPU.
- **A session makes its plan again only when the frame's layout, the black level, the preview's size or the white balance change,** and its JPEG encoder only when the quality does. A `Preview` reports the quality its encoder used.
- **Each stage's time:** `convert`, the frame to a mosaic and cropped; `isp`, the live recipe with its copies to and from the GPU; `jpeg`; and `total`, the frame taken to the JPEG made.
- **The statistics come from the raw mosaic on the CPU,** not the preview, so they see the camera's counts: the histogram's bins run evenly from 0 to the full scale of the frame's bits, and a site is saturated at the full scale. The profiles are worked out 2 × 2 cell by cell, red, the mean of the greens and blue, so they don't jump between colours.
- **The focus value is Brenner's measure on green,** the mean squared difference between greens two sites apart along a row, over the full scale squared. P1.16's autofocus chooses its own measure; this one is for the live view.
- **No transport yet:** each preview goes to a callback; P1.14b puts it on a WebSocket.

## Tests, and the mutants

- **16 tests:**
  - **the bus:** the newest frame and the rest counted; a slow latest-wins reader dropping frames, never holding its publisher; a slow lossless reader slowing its publisher, losing nothing; a lossless queue's length and its publisher's waits; a dropped subscriber freeing a waiting publisher and let go; the first frame after a time, and a timeout;
  - **the preview:** the fit, the binning and the recipe; a session's previews of its viewport's size, their grey level and each stage's time; parameters, a region, the white balance and the link changed mid-stream; the maximum rate and the last frame; frames slower than the maximum rate each previewed; the rate and the means over the last second; bad parameters refused; a session stopped or dropped delivering nothing more;
  - **the statistics:** against the reference; a sharper frame's higher focus; the bins, the least frame, a row or column beyond the frame; the live thread's pace and new parameters, and nothing delivered once it is stopped or dropped;
  - **`tetera-api`:** the camera in the binned live mode at the start.
- **Broken on purpose,** as the spec asks:
  - **the mailbox,** made to keep its oldest frame rather than replace it: three tests failed, the newest frame not reaching its reader;
  - **the backpressure,** a full queue made to drop its new frame rather than wait: two tests failed, frames lost.
- **Run 15 times,** both crates' tests, `tetera-acquire`'s and `tetera-api`'s, passed each time.
- **Mutants:**
  - **the first run,** 321 tested: 58 missed and 4 timeouts, which count as caught (a publisher waiting for ever, a focus loop that never ends). The misses were:
    - the subscribers let go, a lossless queue's length and waits, a receive's deadline, the bus as a camera's sink;
    - the parameters' checks at their edges (a gain of zero or infinity, a region too narrow one way only, 16 pixels exactly);
    - the fit and the binning when only one way binds;
    - a session's parameters, its last error, its stop and drop, its stages' times in milliseconds, its rate and means, and its encoder made again for a new quality;
    - the statistics' least frame, the bins' edges, a row or column beyond the frame, and the live thread stopped or dropped.
  - **Tests now cover them.** The rate and the means moved into a function of their own, `window`, tested with instants of its own. The histogram's bins dropped a cap that nothing could reach. Four are excluded, with their reasons in `.cargo/mutants.toml`:
    - the fit at exactly the viewport's shape, where both branches give the viewport;
    - a session's or the statistics' `stop` emptied, which dropping it at its end does the same;
    - the statistics' pace, `<` or `<=` at the very instant the next is due.
  - **The last run,** 318 tested: 288 caught, 26 unviable, 4 timeouts, **none missed**.
